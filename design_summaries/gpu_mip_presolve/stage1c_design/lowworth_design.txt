Judgement. The three designs agree on the main point, and I checked it in the source: HiGHS's original row-dual bounds are only 0 or infinite (H/HPresolve.cpp:146-149), so every implied bound the five rules read is cost over coefficient of one non-integer column that has at most one lock in a direction (H/HPresolve.cpp:657-725, 3629-3631), and the state needs no bounds on row duals. They also agree that each rule is stated as an exchange with a forward map that the crush replay performs.

Base: validity_first. Its firing conditions are exact in terms of our state with tolerances, every price is verified again at the moment it is used, its checks are designed before its rules, and its oracle extension (rows with several continuous singleton columns, solved by vertex enumeration) does not share an algorithm with the rules it tests.

Grafted from dual_state: the proof that the implied premises of one compensated fixing cannot lean on each other (a compensator whose bound is implied sits in a row with two finite sides, and such a row holds no second moving column; I re-derived it), with both facts asserted at application; two best prices per row and side, so that a column that is the best compensator of its own row is tested with the second; the event-driven choice of the rows to scan and of the candidates; the family serving its lists until they are empty inside one call; one record with roles for the row exchanges of rules 3 and 5, whose crush needs no saved row; dry-run counts before any rule is applied; the bound on the column scale.
Grafted from instances_first: the table of instances that depend on each rule and the milestone order by measured worth; the reference runs and counters of L0; continuous columns only as sources and compensators, which is the plan's guard (PLAN/DESIGN.md:488); the per-milestone test that no instance gets larger; the circular case as a fixed must-not-fire test.

Rejected, each after a look at the source or the data. (a) instances_first's rule 4 without a type change (a gate inside dual bound strengthening plus result flags): it is not the conversion the owner asked for, and the conversion is provable and checkable (equation test at every feasible point; weak test by rounding one column; types clause, crush integrality, an integer line column in the oracle). (c) instances_first's limit of one implied premise per fixing as the built form: the proof shows the premises are independent; the count is reported and the limit is one constant if the owner wants the letter of the plan's guard. (d) validity_first's integer columns as sources and compensators: against the plan's guard and beyond HiGHS for true integers; kept as a named extension. (e) validity_first's three record kinds and the refill crush with a saved row for rules 3 and 5. (f) dual_state's hint per row that outlives calls, as part of the first build: a stale hint loses reductions silently; it is the named remedy if L2 measures the scans as too slow. DUAL STATE
Decision: state_t gets no bound on a row dual, no source and no reset. What the rules need is a function of the state, computed on demand, kept for one pass of the family and verified again at every use.

1. What HiGHS keeps (read). Original bounds by row sense only: no lower side gives y <= 0, no upper side gives y >= 0 (H/HPresolve.cpp:146-149); they change only to 0 or infinity (:975-1011). Implied bounds with one source column each, written by updateRowDualImpliedBounds (H/HPresolve.cpp:684-725) for a column that is not of type integer (:3629-3631), whose bound on one side is infinite or strictly implied (:257-267; for a singleton column implied within the tolerance), and whose 'Orig' dual sum has at most one infinite term (:657-683). The value is (cost - Orig residual without the row) / coefficient (:697-698), kept below 1e7 (:700-702). Five resets keep it true (:886-941, 961-1011). A second writer (:3546-3578) sets a bound of 0 on a one-sided row; that equals the original bound of the row, adds nothing to a reduced-cost sum and only marks the row as a pivot row of a free column substitution, which is rule 8 and not one of the five.

2. What that state is in our terms (inferred from that code and from the lock definition, G/src/state_ops.cpp:88-99). A finite Orig residual is 0, and the number of infinite terms of an Orig dual sum is a lock count of our state (G/include/gprs/state.hpp:246-249). So every implied bound the five rules read is c_j / a_rj of one continuous or implied-integer column j that no row but r holds in one direction. In primal words: j can move the activity of r by one unit at the price |c_j| / |a_rj|, with the sign of the cost, hurting no other row.

3. Definitions. lock_from(row, a, d): the entry a of that row locks its column in column direction d; for d = down, a > 0 with a finite lower side or a < 0 with a finite upper side; for d = up the mirror. A live continuous column j with entry a_ij is a compensator of row i in row direction s (raise or lower the activity), moving in column direction d (up when s is raise and a_ij > 0 or s is lower and a_ij < 0, else down), when: (C1) the lock count of j in direction d, less 1 if lock_from(i, a_ij, d), is 0; (C2) the bound of j in direction d is IEEE infinity, or lock_from(i, a_ij, d) holds and the bound is implied by row i: the candidate of the side of i that holds the lock, over the explicit bounds of the other columns (candidate(), G/src/rules_propagation.cpp:106-132), moved outward by candidate_slack (:140-152), is within the implied_tolerance of N (two candidate roundings) of the bound, with the error guard and one fresh summation. Its price is p = (c_j for d = up, -c_j for d = down) / |a_ij|, admissible when finite and |p| <= price_limit = tol.dual / tol.side_error (1e7). It is 'free' without a lock from i and 'locked' with one. Two facts follow and are asserted at application in the assertion build: a locked compensator used to pay for a column that row i locks sits in a row with two finite sides (its move pushes towards the side opposite to the one that locks the fixed column); a locked compensator can serve row i only, and a row with two finite sides holds no other compensator, because such a row locks each of its columns both ways and C1 would fail.

4. What is stored, and where. New internal header G/src/row_prices.hpp (namespace gprs::detail): price_t {int32_t col; int64_t slot; double price; bool locked}, row_prices_t with the two best entries for raise and the two best for lower (smallest price, ties to the smaller column index; col = -1 for none), bool is_compensator(st, row, slot, direction, out) in O(1), and scan_row_prices(st, row, out) in one pass over the used slots of the row after refresh_activity. The family's work data holds a stamp per row (the number of the pass) and the row_prices_t of the rows scanned in this pass. A slot is valid inside a pass because no rule of the family moves or deletes an entry and compaction happens between calls (G/include/gprs/rules.hpp:80-82). Nothing of it enters state_hash: it is a function of the state. Memory: 8 bytes per row plus about 100 bytes per row scanned in the pass.

5. From which columns. Continuous columns only (the plan's guard, PLAN/DESIGN.md:488). HiGHS also takes implied-integer columns (H/HPresolve.cpp:3630); here a converted column is an integer for every rule and stops being a source. The family counts, per instance, the entries that pass every test but the type (statistic blocked_by_type); decision 4 names the extension.

6. Invalidation. None exists that could be forgotten. A rule that uses a cached entry calls is_compensator again on the state of that moment: the column is live and continuous, C1 on the lock counts of now, C2 with the activities of now. Inside a pass the family fixes columns (which changes no lock of another column and only tightens activities) and turns rows into equations (which adds locks). A verification that fails skips the candidate for this pass; the operation that made the entry stale has listed its rows and columns (fix_col pushes row_event::entries, G/src/state_ops.cpp:303; change_side_locks pushes col_event::locks, :218-232), and the next pass of the same call takes them up.

7. Which rows are scanned and which columns are tested (event-driven; no pass over all rows or columns after the first call). The family has a row list fed by row_event::activity, activity_unread, side and entries, and a column list fed by col_event::locks and N's col_event::entries; at construction every row and column is on them. One pass: (a) take both lists; (b) rows to scan = the listed live rows; plus, for a listed live column with exactly one lock in a direction, that lock row (one walk of the column); plus the row of a listed singleton column; plus all rows of a listed continuous column with no lock in a direction and an infinite bound there (it prices every row it is in); (c) each such row once, in index order: one scan gives its prices and, from the same scan, rule 2, then rule 5 if it is an equation, then rule 3 if it has exactly one finite side; (d) candidates of rule 1 = the listed columns plus the free columns of the scanned rows that have a price, in index order, each once; a locking row of a candidate that was not scanned in this pass is scanned when it is first asked. The family repeats passes while its lists are not empty and the budget allows, so a chain inside the family costs no round. The budget ({8, 4, 100000}) is tested between two rows and between two columns; unvisited rows and columns go back on the lists.

8. Why every new opportunity is seen (derived by reading G/src/state_ops.cpp; the second-presolve tests are its test). A change of cost, locks or entries of a column lists the column; a bound change of a column lists all its rows (set_col_bound, :195-215); a side or an entry of a row lists the row. A column k becomes fixable only if a premise changed: its own cost, locks or entries (k is listed); its target bound (all its rows are listed, and since every locking row must have a price, one of them has one, which makes k a candidate); or a price of a locking row, through the cost or locks of the compensator (the compensator is listed and sends its lock row or all its rows) or through the implied test (an activity or side event of that row).

9. Cost. Per pass at most the entries of the scanned rows, one walk per listed column with a single lock, and one walk per candidate that ends at the first locking row without a price. A row is scanned at most once per pass. In the assertion build the cache of a pass is compared with a fresh scan at the end of the pass.

10. Implied bounds, rounds on explicit bounds, make_explicit. No recorded implied bound (state_t::implied_lower and its fellows) is read; 'implied' is always the candidate of the one lock row, recomputed from the stored activities over explicit bounds. Before make_explicit a continuous column has its input bounds, as in HiGHS. After it, a free compensator still has an infinite bound (no row can imply one), and a locked one has at best the candidate of its lock row moved outward as explicit bound, which passes with implied_tolerance; so the decision is the same before and after the switch and in a second presolve. The test is weak where HiGHS asks for a strict margin; that is valid here because every use is one exchange through that same row and the bound stays in the model (see the rules). One loss against HiGHS: a bound that make_explicit wrote from a row that was deleted since is finite and not implied (also true for R2 and R5 of N; PLAN/stage1c_design/critiques.txt:122); L1 counts such columns.

11. Fixed-point flag. The flag additionally requires that neither new family is waiting, stopped or limited. Every decision is a function of the state (smallest price, ties by index, named constants); nothing is left to the history of a row or column; every premise has an event (item 8). The only report of limited is the saved-row limit of N. Not covered, as today: the last bits of a stored activity.

12. Rules that read no price. Rule 2 reads one source directly from the entry it scans. Rules 3 and 5 read lock counts, cost signs, explicit bounds and compensated sums of one row. Rule 4 reads types, coefficients, sides and bounds. HiGHS reads its implied-equation predicate in the implied-integer tests as well (H/HPresolveImpliedInts.cpp:28-34, 99-105); here the row is an equation in the state before those tests run.

13. If L2 measures the scans of rows without a price as too slow, the remedy is dual_state's hint (one byte per row: no compensator at the last scan), kept across calls, refreshed by every scan, verified against a fresh scan of every live row in the assertion build. It changes no result when every premise has an event; it is not in the first build because a stale hint loses reductions without a trace in a release run.

  condition: Not in primal-only mode. Column k is live, of any type. Both directions are tried, down first when cost_k >= 0, else up first. Direction D has the target v = col_lower[k] (down) or col_upper[k] (up). (1) v is finite, |v| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility, and no term a * v of the column counts as infinite (is_infinite_term); an infinite or huge target is left alone and no status is concluded. (2) The lock count of k in direction D is at least 1; with 0 the lock rule owns the column (G/src/rules_dual_fixing.cpp:39-41). (3) One walk over the live entries (i, a) of k. A locking row without such a price ends the test. (4) The rate R = s * cost_k - sum over the locking rows of price_i * |a|, with s = +1 for down and -1 for up, as a compensated sum (dd_add_product). The column is fixed when R >= -tol.cancellation * (|cost_k| + sum of |price_i * a|). Effect: fix_column_compensated(st, ps, k, v, terms) next to fix_column in G/src/rules_bounds.cpp, which pushes the record and calls st.fix_col; the terms are {j_i, a_ik / a_ij}. In the assertion build the two facts of dual_state item 3 are asserted for every locked compensator. The family counts the fixings by the number of compensators with a finite implied bound (0, 1, 2 or more) and the candidates that fail only on an infinite target with R > 0 (statistic dominated_unbounded).
  validity: Class D, an exchange with a recorded compensation; off in primal-only mode. Proof: from a feasible point move x_k to v, a change of delta in direction D, and move every compensator j_i by |a_ik / a_ij| * delta in its direction. (i) A row that does not lock k in D only gains from the move of k. (ii) A locking row i with a free compensator has one finite side; the compensator restores what k took; every other compensator in that row has no lock from it in its direction and so moves the activity away from the finite side. (iii) A locking row i with a locked compensator has two finite sides; its only moving columns are k and that compensator (a row with two finite sides locks each of its columns both ways, so C1 would fail for any other compensator in it), and the two moves cancel in it; its activity and the values of its other columns are unchanged. (iv) Any other row that holds a compensator does not lock it in its direction (C1). (v) A free compensator has an infinite bound in its direction. A locked one has a bound that is infinite or implied by its row i; after the exchange row i holds and every other column of it is inside its explicit bounds (k sits on its bound), so the compensator is inside its bound up to implied_tolerance. Each implied premise is used only in its own row, where nothing else moved, so the premises cannot lean on each other; the circular case (x - y <= 0, y - x <= 0) gives no compensator at all, because neither column passes C1. (vi) The objective changes by -delta * R, which is not positive. An integer k lands on an integral bound; compensators are continuous. The structure check cannot see a restriction, and all five rules are restrictions; so the oracle (outcome and optimal value) and the crush of feasible points decide, and the structure check guards the stack, the types and the column map. Every record kind arrives in the milestone of its rule together with its reader in the structure check, its crush step with unit tests of crush alone, and wrong engines in G/check/make_mutants.py; a wrong engine that the named check does not catch stops the milestone. All generator knobs default to 0, so old seeds give old cases.

(1) Oracle class, group rows (G/tests/oracle.hpp, oracle.cpp). Today a row holds at most one line column (oracle.hpp:12-23), so stuffing over continuous singletons, the single-equation rule with several continuous compensators and a wrong weak implied-integer test in a row with a second continuous column cannot be tested exactly. Extension: a row may hold several line columns when each is continuous, has two finite bounds and has no entry in another row; at most three per row; such a row holds no other line column. For every assignment of the enumerated columns solve() treats the row as a one-row linear program over the group and enumerates its vertices: every member at a bound, or all but one at a bound and the row tight at a finite side. With finite bounds there is no ray, so the best vertex is the optimum. classify() and into_class() accept such rows; solution_t::feasible takes the best vertex and far the worst. The solver is tested against a search on a grid of the members (G/tests/test_oracle_brute.cpp) and against the old solver on a campaign of old seeds (same verdicts on every model of the old class) before any rule uses it. If the repair drops the replay, rules 1, 2, 3 and 5 lose their solver-free test and this design does not stand. stack_consistent and postsolve_hash open to new kinds; an unknown kind an error in compose_records. My comp_fix with one term is N's parallel_fix {col, value, kept, ratio}: I recommend one kind with a term list, R6 pushing one term, and the size guard on the fixed value that the critic asked for (PLAN/stage1c_design/critiques.txt:13-15) in the shared helper. Groundwork of N's first milestone that my rules lean on: the oracle's crush condition 'feasible and not above, equal when dual_findings == 0'; the argument and the default-mode verdicts of the crush check; the planted-point test with the replay; result_t::dual_findings and a finding counter per sub-rule; the second presolve on a sample in gprs_fuzz; the objective test of the structure check, which must carry the scale of col_scale.

Version 2's amendments (A.2) ask its groundwork to leave room for more families, record kinds and list bits and for a third column type; this design uses the first three and no third column type in the state (decision 5).

6. Types. N's statement 'no type change' ends with rule 4. N's families must read the type through state_t::is_integer() at application and keep no type-dependent data across calls without an event; the events of set_col_integer list the column and its rows on N's lists. The shared alias code and the merge must read postsolve_t::col_integer, not the input types, in their assertions. R3 (continuous columns only) and the continuous-kept case of the merge end for a converted column; this is why implied_integer_t runs last in the round.

7. R7. Rule 4 reaches supportcase42 only through R7 on the converted column. Required of the repaired R7: it runs for a column with cost >= 0 and at least one down lock whatever its up locks are (the validity critic's third defect; version 2 says it repaired this), and it treats a converted column as an integer.

8. Which rule owns what, so that no reduction is claimed twice. A column without a lock in its cost direction is the lock rule's. A zero-cost column with an infinite bound and no lock in that direction is R4's (HiGHS's forcing column, nested in the weak test at H/HPresolve.cpp:3701-3745), so it is normally gone before it would price rows. A singleton source whose two ends are implied is R5's, which removes the row; rule 2 asks only for the bound in the cost direction, keeps the row as an equation and applies to columns of any length. R7 on a singleton column is the first test of stuffing with one candidate; R6 fixes a singleton column when another singleton of the row with an infinite or implied bound can absorb it; stuffing takes several candidates with finite capacities. These orders are for economy, never for validity: with any of N's rules switched off mine stay correct.

9. Oracle. N extends the class by twin pairs of continuous parallel columns; my group rows are a second extension. classify() accepts a row with at most one line column, a twin pair, or a group; two continuous singleton columns of one row are both a twin pair and a group, and the two solvers must agree on them, which is a test.

10. Bound changes through set_col_lower / set_col_upper only (the widened bounds of a merge included) and side changes through set_row_lower / set_row_upper, so that every row of the column and every column of the row is listed; the event argument of dual_state item 8 depends on it.

11. What I need to know from the design in force before L0 starts: the final rule and list indices, the form of the saved-row pool, the milestone in which crush becomes a replay, and whether parallel_fix becomes a term list.

RISKS
- Nothing was run in the engine. The exchange proofs (the compensated fixing with several compensators, the single-equation rule, both tests of stuffing, the weak implied-integer test) were derived by the designers and re-derived by me by reading. dual_state reports an exact-arithmetic scratch test of its own conditions on tiny random MIPs without a lost optimum; I did not rerun it, and it has no tolerances, no crush maps and no scaling. The oracle campaign of each milestone is the first test of the engine's form.
- Reach is inferred from counters and sizes; the model files are not on this machine. That the compensated form covers b1c1s1's fixings, that the weak test is what converts on supportcase42 and that the chain conversion, R7, singleton substitution carries over, and that csched007, snowy and map10 need two-entry rows turned into equations, are inferences. L0 adds the counters and the trace that confirm or refute them before rule code is written.
- The worth of rules 1 and 5 together is in no stored run: HiGHS has no switch for the single-equation part, off_leftout.csv leaves it on, and each of the two takes over the other's fixings (neos-827175, savsched1, irish-electricity). The figure 1.0006 for 'all five off' is therefore a lower bound of what is lost without them. The order L3, L4 rests on the bound from off_dual_fixing_substitution.csv and may change after the L0 passes.
- Narrower than HiGHS in three places, each unmeasured: implied-integer columns are no sources and no compensators (a converted column is an integer here); a bound that make_explicit wrote from a row that was deleted since is not implied; tolerances are stricter (rounding only in rules 3 and 5, the sign of the reduced-cost bound exact to 1e-12 relative). Instances with non-integer data can lose firings. L0 counts the first, L1 the second.
- Rule 1 uses one implied premise per locking row with a locked compensator, where the plan's guard says at most one per derivation. The proof that they are independent rests on two structural facts. If an engine change ever lets a rule move two columns of one such row, or a row with two finite sides stop locking a column both ways, the argument breaks; the family asserts both facts at application, in the assertion build only.
- The implied test is weak, with implied_tolerance (about 2e-7 at unit size): after an exchange a source or a compensator can pass its bound by that much, and a later reduction can enlarge it. False alarms of the crush check on real instances are possible and have to be traced, not waived.
- The type change is the first write of col_type after construction. Every family written under 'no type change' is affected: R3, the type rules of the parallel columns, the root choice of the alias forest, the push assertions of the stack on input types, the marks of bound propagation. A family that keeps type-dependent data without an event would act on a stale type. Need 6, the mutant ev_type and the rerun of the nine rules' tests in L3 address it.
- A conversion can make an instance larger. In HiGHS blp-ic98 and blp-ar98 end 3% larger with the conversion on, and physiciansched3-3 loses more with implied equations off and the conversion on than with both off. Here a converted column additionally loses its roles as source, compensator and R3 column. L3 stops on any instance that grows; the remedy is a default of the sub-rule switches, not a change of the rule.
- Crush now holds real logic: a saved-row evaluation (tight_row), linear compensation (comp_fix), two exchanges with roles (row_exchange), a rounding and a scaling. Three checks lean on it, and a crush error looks like a rule error on a point that is not optimal. Unit tests of crush alone and one wrong engine per record that skips or bends the replay are the safeguard. All of it depends on the forward replay of the nine-rule design.
- The oracle gets new solver code (vertex enumeration for group rows) next to the twin split of the nine-rule design. An error there passes wrong engines or fails right ones; it is tested against a grid search and against the old solver on old seeds before any rule uses it. Rule 1 with a continuous fixed column and rule 5 with compensators that have further rows stay outside the class and rest on planted points and the real instances.
- Fixed-point flag. It rests on the event list of dual_state item 8 and on the row cache of rule 4, both derived by reading G/src/state_ops.cpp. A premise without an event loses reductions and the claim, not validity; the second-presolve tests, the sample in the campaign and the assertion-build comparison of the price cache with a fresh scan are the safeguard.
- Rounds. An implied equation reaches the doubleton equations, simple probing and R2 only in the next round, a conversion reaches row tightening and R7 only in the next round, and max_rounds = 20 counts the whole presolve. Chains through the new families can end on that limit and clear the flag; L1 and L3 report the rounds.
- Time. The exchange family takes every activity event, so each round rescans the rows whose activity changed, about the cost of one propagation sweep over the same rows, and a listed column with a single lock pays one walk of its column. After a wave of row deletions that is up to one pass over the nonzeros. Instances with long columns (neos-3402454-bohle, rd-rplusc-21) and with many lock fixings (neos-787933) are the ones to watch; the per-call budget bounds it at the price of a cleared flag when it stops. The stored seconds come from a shared node.
- col_scale changes the meaning of a column index for every later record and by-product, as the merge does. The adapter's column map needs the marker, and any by-product exported in reduced indices must be in scaled units. How often it fires is unknown before the L0 counter exists.
- Solver quality is not tested locally. Turning inequalities into equations and continuous columns into integers changes what the solver sees; cuOpt keeps PaPILO's implied integers as continuous with a list today (the engine reader's fact, not read by me). The milestones measure sizes only.
- Sources. I made no remote read of PaPILO. The repaired nine-rule design is on disk and in force; I read only its amendments and five items of its driver section, so a difference in a part I did not read (its check extensions, its R7 text) can change a need. Counters that I did not sum myself are marked as the designers'.

DECISIONS FOR THE OWNER
- Row-dual bounds as state. The plan says the five rules need bounds on the row duals as new state (PLAN/DESIGN.md:130-131). All three designers and I store none: read in HiGHS, every such bound is cost over coefficient of one column with at most one lock in a direction, so it is recomputed per row, kept for one pass and verified at every use. This removes HiGHS's five reset events as a source of stale bounds. Recommended: accept and reword that sentence. The alternative is HiGHS's kept bounds, about 24 bytes per row and 44 per column in the state with a reset in about a dozen state operations, mirrored in the device engine.
- Weakly implied bounds in the dual rules. The plan's guard says the bound that drops a side is infinite or strictly implied (PLAN/DESIGN.md:488-489); HiGHS asks for a strict margin except for singleton columns. The exchange proofs need only that the bound holds wherever the lock row holds, and the weak test gives the same answer before and after bounds become explicit. Recommended: the weak test with the tolerance of R2 and R5 of the nine-rule design. The alternative, a strict margin for columns with more than one entry, loses every column whose bound was made explicit from its own row.
- Several implied premises in one dominated-column fixing. The plan's guard says at most one per derivation (PLAN/DESIGN.md:489); instances_first built that limit. Designed here: one per locking row, with the proof that they cannot lean on each other and both structural facts asserted. Recommended: the per-row form; L2 reports how many fixings on the 240 use two or more, and a single named constant restores the limit of one if you want the letter of the guard.
- Sources and compensators are continuous columns only, which is the plan's guard (PLAN/DESIGN.md:488) and is narrower than HiGHS, where implied-integer columns stay sources. validity_first's extension (an integer source when its row is integral for it; an integer compensator when the fixed column is integer and the factor integral) is provable and checkable but relaxes that guard and goes beyond HiGHS for true integers. Recommended: continuous only now; decide on the extension with the L0 counter of source types and the count blocked_by_type of L3.
- Type of a converted column. Designed: integer in the state and in the reduced model, with result_t::implied_integer as a flag 'continuous in the input'; no third column type in the state, although the amendments of version 2 leave room for one and the plan names a type order with implied integer (PLAN/DESIGN.md:493). A third type would only be needed if the adapter is to hand flagged columns to the solver as continuous, which is safe only while the defining equation is still in the reduced model. Say which you want.
- Whether the conversions are on by default. The weak test is a dual reduction and cuts feasible points; the equation test is primal. HiGHS's own run ends 3% larger on blp-ic98 and blp-ar98 with its conversion on. Designed: both on, each with its own switch, the weak test off in primal-only mode, and L3 stops on any instance that grows. The alternative that instances_first designed (no type change; the weak test only as a gate that lets dual bound strengthening tighten a continuous column, plus result flags by the equation test) is valid and reaches supportcase42 by its reading; I did not take it because it is not the conversion you asked for. Say if it is enough.
- Column scaling (HiGHS's transform x = s z in an equation with one continuous column) is part of rule 4 in HiGHS's cheap loop. Designed: built last (L6), only where the equation test fails without it, within a factor of 1024. Its worth cannot be separated in the stored runs; its known price on the 240 is at most physiciansched3-3's 28 rows. Say if it should be dropped or built as HiGHS does (whenever the scale is not 1).
- Stuffing in full or for integer candidates only. The measured need on the 240 is one column on sp97ar, an all-integer model. Designed: the full rule, with the second test in the form the exchange proves, which fixes at least what HiGHS's stricter test fixes. The integer-only form would need no group rows in the oracle for this rule, but the single-equation rule needs them anyway.
- Tolerances stricter than HiGHS, chosen so that the checks need no exception: only rounding in the single-equation and stuffing tests (HiGHS 1e-6; two of the three designers proposed a tenth of the feasibility tolerance), the sign of the reduced-cost bound exact to 1e-12 relative (HiGHS 1e-7), no 'unbounded or infeasible' from a strictly dominated column with an infinite target (HiGHS returns it). Each can be loosened after the table of L6 shows what it costs.
- Order of the milestones. Designed, by worth measured on the 240 and by what each needs: implied equations, dominated column, implied integers, single equation, stuffing, column scaling. The single-equation extension has no measurement; it moves before the implied integers if the L0 pass with both dual rules off shows that the engine needs it for neos-827175, savsched1 or irish-electricity. The task text lists the rules in another order (dominated column first, single equation last); say if that order is meant as binding.
- Reference runs in L0 need two switches and four counters in the instrumented HiGHS and four passes on the 240 (about five minutes each with one job). They are the only way to measure the single-equation extension, the pair of it with the dominated tests, and the forms inferred here. Optional fifth pass: implied row-dual bounds frozen at the original ones, which measures in one run everything that reads them, the pivot rows of rule 8 through other columns included.
- Oracle class. Designed: extended by rows whose continuous columns are singleton columns with finite bounds, solved exactly by vertex enumeration, so that continuous stuffing and the single-equation rule with several compensators are tested exactly. The alternative (dual_state) is no extension and planted points only for those cases.
- Not built, each valid as an exchange and available if you want it: the zero-cost implied equation (a row that can be made tight without loss), which HiGHS uses only to make a row a pivot row of a free column substitution through another column; and the 'unbounded or infeasible' status of rule 1.
- max_rounds stays at 20. The new families feed the other families in the next round; if instances end on the limit through them, raising it is a default change that needs your word.
- Two corrections for the plan. The file off_leftout.csv does not have all five rules off: the single-equation extension is on in it, so the sentence 'switched off together: 1.0006' (PLAN/DESIGN.md:127-130) describes four rules and the forcing column. And the plan's amendment list ends at A8, while the task for this design names A9.
