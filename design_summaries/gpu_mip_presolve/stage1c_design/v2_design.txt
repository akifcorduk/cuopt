What changed against the first design, and why.

1. The checks are written against the structure check that is in the tree. The first design described linear forms, a snapshot chain per row and a count merge_unproven on a file one version behind. I checked the critic's model on the present code: a row that had an equation subtracted is proved from the reduced row of that equation (structure_check.hpp:843), and once a singleton-column rule has taken a column out of that equation the tie is gone and a correct result fails. Version 2 keeps the three proof paths of the file as they are and changes what they are given: a column removed with saved rows is a check variable with the box of its record, a saved row is an anchor (a constraint the undo makes true, with the record's own column in it), and every lookup of a reduced row becomes the first anchor of that row after a stack position. No linear forms are needed. A merge gets two variables and an identity, which gives a verdict where the first design had a count. An objective test is added (the file reads no cost today).

2. Rules repaired where a critic showed a hole, each with its smallest model as a test: the dominance fixing has a size guard; dual bound strengthening asks only for the lock in its own direction; the single-lock alias is evaluated per lock row with one candidate binary (the one with the largest coefficient), so its cost is the row once and not once per column; chains of singleton columns are finished inside one call; the composed pivot ratio of a chain is bounded, not the single one; simple probing collects its aliases in the alias forest and substitutes each column once; the alias of the lock rule names no row; the undo of a merge and the round-trip clause of the crush check have their tolerance cases. One change is my own: a visit of a row removes all its zero-cost singleton columns with one record, because one saved copy of the row per removed column costs the row length times the number of its singleton columns (supportcase12 has 55,681 such columns in HiGHS).

3. No milestone is credited with a gain that no measurement supports. I recomputed everything from the result files. With only measured single-rule losses credited the gate ends at 0.9918 in nonzeros, 0.9874 in rows and 0.9921 in columns; with neos-3402294-bobin reached as well at 0.9988, 0.9994 and 0.9975. The first design's 0.968, 0.985, 0.994, 0.9996, 1.001 are withdrawn. The rows gate passes only if bobin is reached, and the chain that reaches it is a hypothesis; the HiGHS run that decides it is named and needs no engine code. The gate measures nonzeros, rows and columns. Every figure is marked as measured on HiGHS, predicted, or unknown.

4. The column family is split in two (singleton columns; dual rules), so that the O(1) rules have their own list and budget. The singleton-column milestone comes first, simple probing second; the groundwork step is cut to what the first rule milestone needs and starts with reference runs.

5. The oracle's twin extension is exact only for two continuous twins; the generator draws nothing else, and the solver works in the merged column so that a huge bound does not round the objective.

6. Two facts the first design did not have. The plan's amendment A8 was changed after it (P/DESIGN.md:114-127): the nine rules are the required set, the gate measures rows and columns, and the merge of integer pairs is behind a switch; HISTORY Entry 36 then reopened the first of these until the owner has seen the numbers on all 240 instances. And two combined runs now exist: with all left-out rules off together HiGHS keeps 1.0006 times the nonzeros, 1.0015 the rows and 1.0006 the columns (B/cheap_ablation/off_leftout.csv, 19 instances change). The single-equation extension has no switch and is still on in that run, so its price together with the dominated-column test remains unmeasured.

  name: R1. Simple probing on an equation (family simple_probing; milestone M2)
  condition: Row r of row_list::simple_probing is live, row_lower == row_upper == b finite with |b| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility, and row_free_size[r] >= 3 (two free entries are a doubleton equation). After refresh_activity neither activity has an infinite term, so every free column has finite bounds. Quick test in O(1): |(M - b) - (b - m)| less both error bounds is at most tol.feasibility, m and M being the compensated minimum and maximum activity. Then one scan of the row looks for the binary p: a free integer column with col_upper - col_lower == 1 exactly for which both conditional rows are forcing in the engine's own sense: gap_low = (b - m) - |a_p| and gap_high = (M - b) - |a_p| each pass the test of forcing_row (G/src/rules_rows.cpp:152-180) evaluated on the free entries other than p: |gap| + error <= tol.feasibility * min|a_i| / max(1, max|a_i|), and with a continuous column among them the gap may be positive only by rounding (the error bound plus forcing_rounding_units rounding units of the terms). The smallest column index among several. Every other free column i has u_i - l_i <= alias_scale_limit = 1e6 (a named constant and a function of the state). The row then gives one alias per other free column: x_i = v_low_i + (v_high_i - v_low_i) * (x_p - l_p), where v_high_i is the bound the row forces on i when x_p = l_p + 1 (for a_p > 0 the minimum-activity bound of i: l_i if a_i > 0, else u_i) and v_low_i its other bound; a_p < 0 exchanges the two. Batch (changed): the aliases of the listed rows of one call are collected as edges of the alias forest that the doubleton family uses, by code shared with it. A row is taken into the batch only when no two of its free columns are already connected in the forest; a row that is not taken goes back on the list. After freeze() every column is substituted once into its root, a row is deleted only when every one of its edges became a map, and a row with a cut edge stays with the columns that were substituted and is listed again by its entries event. Bounds are read before the first substitution; the transfer of bounds to the root is the shared code and changes nothing where the root is the binary.
  validity: Class B/S, primal; on in primal-only mode. Each alias holds at every feasible point: the term of p takes one of two values that differ by |a_p|; with the larger one the rest of the row must equal its minimum activity plus gap_low, with the smaller one its maximum activity minus gap_high; both gaps are zero within the forcing tolerance, so each other column sits at the bound named above. This is the conclusion the engine draws for a forcing row, with the same tolerance and the same restriction to rounding-size gaps when a continuous column is in the row (G/src/rules_rows.cpp:140-151). HiGHS tests an absolute 1e-9 (H/HPresolve.cpp:2696-2698, read), which is not small for a column with |a_i| = 1e-6; it is not copied. Equalities that hold at every feasible point can be composed and oriented either way, so the batch is valid for any root the forest picks; an integer column gets an integral scale and offset because its bounds are integral, and the forest cuts an edge that is not admissible (G/include/gprs/postsolve.hpp:149-169). Activities are over explicit bounds only (HiGHS also reads implied bounds); a row that balances only through an implied bound of a continuous column fires in the rounds after make_explicit.
  postsolve: Existing alias record with the new field origin = probing and row = r (the row leaves the model). Undo x_i = scale * x_rep + offset as today. Crush: nothing; every feasible point satisfies the alias. The deleted equation leaves no record: with the aliases composed its coefficient on the root is zero or a rounding-size term, which the rows test of the structure check reads as it is (structure_check.hpp:936-973).
  cost: O(1) per listed row for the quick test, where most rows end; one row scan for the binary; then the live entries of the substituted columns and of their roots, once per call, spent on the family's budget {4, 2, 1e5}. The stop is tested between two rows while the batch is collected, never inside its application. Within a call an entry moves at most once. Over the presolve the bound is the nonzeros times the number of calls in which the root of a column is substituted again (at most max_rounds), not 'the nonzeros' as the first design said; the alias depth is reported at M2. - [6]
  name: R6. Parallel columns: fixing by dominance (family parallel_cols; milestone M3)
  condition: Not in primal-only mode. Detection: per live column a key (number of live entries; hash over row and coefficient divided by the coefficient in the smallest row index), recomputed for columns whose entries changed. A bucket is the set of columns with equal key in index order. A singleton column has its row as key, so all singleton columns of one row form one bucket, as in HiGHS (H/HPresolveParallelRowsAndCols.cpp:38-40, 75-76, read). Exact test of a pair p < q: the same live rows and one ratio s = a_q/a_p with |a_q - s * a_p| <= tol.zero * |a_q| in every row and |a_q - s * a_p| * R <= tol.activity_error_share * tol.feasibility, R being the largest finite |bound| of the column that is fixed. D = cost_q - s * cost_p as a compensated value; the costs count as equal when |D| <= tol.cancellation * (|cost_q| + |s * cost_p|). Moving delta from q to p (x_q - delta, x_p + s * delta) changes no row and changes the cost by -delta * D. Cases in this order: (a) D >= 0 or equal: q is fixed at its lower bound when p can absorb s * (x_q - l_q); (b) D <= 0 or equal: q is fixed at its upper bound when p can absorb in the other direction; (c) and (d): the same with the roles exchanged (ratio 1/s). Size guard (new): the value v the column is fixed at satisfies |v| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility and no term a * v of the column counts as infinite (is_infinite_term); a bound that fails this is treated like an infinite one: the pair is left and marked. 'Can absorb in a direction': the bound of the absorber in that direction is infinite, or one of its rows implies it: the candidate of that row over the explicit bounds of its other columns (O(1) from the stored activities, moved outward by candidate_slack) is within implied_tolerance of the bound; the rows of the absorber are scanned and the first that implies is taken. - singleton_cols_t serves its column list and its row list until both stay empty or the budget stops it. A listed column that is a singleton leads to a visit of its row; a listed row is visited; a row is visited at most once per pass over the lists (a stamp per row). A visit scans the row once, finds its live singleton columns and applies, in this order: R2 when the row is an equation, R5 when it is not, each for the smallest column index that passes, and otherwise R3 for all its zero-cost continuous singleton columns at once. A listed column that is no singleton is tested for R4 in O(1). The stop is tested between two visits; unvisited indices go back on the lists.
- dual_cols_t takes its column list and its row list. R7: the listed live columns and the live columns of the listed rows, each once per call, in index order. Then R8: the listed rows and the lock rows of the listed columns with a single lock, each once per call, in index order.
- parallel_cols_t takes its column list (keys of these columns are recomputed) and, only while a left-alone mark exists, its row list, from which it lists the marked columns of the listed rows; R6 then R9 for every listed column with a bucket neighbour.

8. Round (src/presolve.cpp:265-288): cascade, parallel rows, doubleton equations as today, then simple probing, singleton columns, dual columns, parallel columns, each followed by end_phase(). The lock rule stays in the cascade and so runs before the column rules of the same state. The new phases come after the Stage 1 phases so that the first round does on the input model what Stage 1 does: the engine is ahead of the reference on nine instances through the row addition, which reads singleton columns that R2 and R5 remove. The families run in the rounds before and after make_explicit. fixed_terms_matter() (:293-297) and the renewed test after absorb_fixed_terms (:354) include the four families, which read sides and activities; phase_hash gets seven entries per round.

9. Work over the whole presolve. Each family has a per-call budget and is called at most once per round; max_rounds = 20 counts the rounds of the whole presolve (include/gprs/presolve.hpp:97, src/presolve.cpp:268). Bounds per call, in entries: R1 the listed rows once and the substituted columns once; the singleton family the visited rows once per pass and, for a chain, the entries of the rows it removes once; R7 the listed rows once and the candidate columns once; R8 the visited rows once and the listed columns once; the parallel columns the listed columns times twice the bucket limit. No cap depends on history except the three that report limited (item 10). Work and seconds per family are reported per instance at every milestone against the ceilings of the gate.

10. Fixed-point claim. result_t::fixed_point is the present expression (src/presolve.cpp:357-358) and additionally requires that none of the four families is waiting. With the flag set a second presolve finds nothing for R1 to R9 either. It rests on three things. (a) Every test reads the state as it is, never a record or the history of a row. (b) Every premise has an event: cost, locks and entries of a column through the column events, scale_row included; sides, entries and both activities of a row, and through them the bounds of every column of the row, through the row lists. (c) The caps that stop a rule are functions of the state (alias_scale_limit, the single pivot ratio, the size guards, the choice of the largest binary and of the smallest index); those that depend on history report limited: comparisons of a column that end on parallel_bucket_limit, more absorbers in a singleton bucket than that limit, the saved-row limit, and a substitution refused only because of the growth factor of its column. A column that R8 skips because it became a target in this call is on the list again by its own event. A call that stops on its budget returns its lists. The implied tests allow two candidate roundings, so they give the same answer before and after make_explicit. Not covered, as today: the last bits of a stored activity. The tests that run a second presolve (tests/test_engine_fixed_point.cpp, test_e2e_presolve.cpp, test_e2e_planted.cpp:287-289) test this paragraph, and the campaign tool gets a second presolve on a sample (it has none: tools/gprs_fuzz.cpp:318 is its only call of presolve).

11. Determinism. No hash container; buckets are sorted keys; candidates are processed in index order; ties go to the smaller column index (the binary of R1, the binary and the columns of R8, the kept column of R9, the absorber of R6, the removed column of R2 and R5). The columns of an R3 record are in increasing index, so the undo does not depend on the storage order of the row. New lists and arrays enter state_hash.

CHECKS
Written against the files as they are in the tree: G/check/structure_check.hpp (1062 lines, reads row_addition records), G/check/crush_check.hpp (448 lines), G/check/gprs_check.cpp, G/tests/oracle.hpp and oracle.cpp, G/tests/test_e2e_planted.cpp, G/tools/gprs_fuzz.cpp, G/check/make_mutants.py (49 wrong engines: 13 m_, 23 r_, 13 a_).

Principle. Every built rule has a forward map. For the primal rules crush keeps the point; for the dual rules the replay performs the exchange (R6 by its record, R7 by the clip, R5, R8 and every dual fixing by dropping the column). So for any feasible point x of the original model the crushed point is feasible in the reduced model and its objective is not above that of x, in default mode too, and equal when result.dual_findings == 0. With the postsolve contract this proves that the optimum is kept. Each check extension arrives in the milestone of the rule it reads, with wrong engines in make_mutants.py that the named check must catch; a wrong engine that is not caught stops the milestone.

A. Structure check: how it works today, in the terms the extension uses. compose_records (:216-325) tests the stack and gives every original column a map: a constant, or scale * (one reduced column) + offset. For every original row the composed function g of the reduced columns is tested in one of three ways: against several reduced rows together, its own and those of the equations it is made of, by a search over multipliers of a weak-duality bound (:859-934, combined_t :440-454, visit_vertices :588-704); against its own reduced row alone, or that of the row it was merged into, by the smallest of the one-multiplier bounds (:975-1039, implied_maximum :340-385); or, with no reduced row, over the bounds (:936-973). Which reduced row stands for a row is decided by reduced_row and final_row (:790-804), and for an equation at :843. A bound is a proof whatever the multipliers are, so any constraint that is known to hold at the postsolved point may be offered as a row.

B. The extension, in general. (1) Check variables: the reduced columns, then one variable per column of a col_from_rows record with the box [col_lower, col_upper] of the record, and two per merge record. The map of a column stays 'constant or scale * (one check variable) + offset'; the first design's sparse linear forms are not needed. (2) Anchors: a saved row is a constraint over check variables that holds at every postsolved point, because the undo makes it hold; its variables include the record's own columns. Reduced rows are anchors at the end of the stack. (3) Positions: every record has its index in the order array. The reverse pass of compose_records (:312-323) composes a saved row when it reaches its record, with the maps as they are then, which are the meanings of the column indices at that position; only afterwards does it give the record's own columns their variables. (4) next_anchor(row, t) replaces reduced_row[final_row(row)]: the first saved row of that row at a position after t; else its reduced row; else, when a parallel record at position p removed it, next_anchor(kept row, p) (the loop guard of :798-800 stays); else none. (5) An obligation is a function with sides at a position; it is tested by the three paths above with these rows: its own next anchor, and for every row_addition record on its row between its position and the position of that anchor (to the end without one) the anchor next_anchor(equation, q) of the record's equation at the record's position q, continued through the records on that equation between q and the position of its anchor; row_equations_t (:393-436) keeps the records per row with their positions for this walk and still stops at max_row_equations rows. Without any col_from_rows or merge record every anchor is a reduced row and every window is the whole stack, so the check does what it does today.

C. How each record kind fits.
- Alias with an origin. doubleton and probing: as today, the row is marked gone (:282). The equation of a probing alias composes to a zero or rounding-size coefficient on the root and passes the rows test. lock: row = -1 and no row is marked gone, so a later row_addition on the lock row is accepted (:301 would reject it today). An alias is a restriction; the rows that held the column are proved against their reduced rows with multiplier 1. A lock alias in a primal-only result is a stack error; structure_check gets the mode as a third argument (it is called at gprs_check.cpp:382 and gprs_fuzz.cpp:325).
- A column defined by a saved row (col_from_rows, defined; R2, R5). The column is a check variable whose box is the explicit bounds at the push. Its saved row is a terminal anchor, an equation, valid without a condition. Obligations: the pivot row of the original model is tested against that anchor (its g is a multiple of it, so it passes with one multiplier and lies inside the sides, for R5 at the tight side); the box is tested from the saved row alone: the largest and smallest value of the variable subject to the equation and the boxes of the saved columns, with its own bounds taken as infinite, by implied_maximum with the multipliers 0 and 1/a, must lie inside the box (this is the engine's implied-free test recomputed; counters box_tested, box_failures); an integer column needs integer saved columns, integral ratios a_i/a and an integral side/a (integral_failures). A row that held the column earlier and was deleted then is tested over the box, as the engine argued when it deleted it. The dual flag in a primal-only result is a stack error.
- Columns removed from a row that stays (interval; R3). The columns are check variables with their boxes; the saved row is an anchor that holds if the widened row holds. Obligation at the record's position: the entries of the saved row without the record's columns, with the sides L' and U' that the check recomputes from the record, proved from the next anchor of that row and its equations after that position (counters snapshot_tested, snapshot_untested, snapshot_failures). The original row is proved against its first anchor, which is this saved row, with the record's columns as variables of the proof. An integer column in such a record is a stack error.
- A forcing column (forcing; R4). A check variable with its box; its saved rows are terminal anchors valid by existence, which the stack test decides from the record alone: every saved row bounds the column from one and the same side (its coefficient and its one finite side), and the box is infinite on the other side.
- A fixing by dominance (parallel_fix). Read as a fixed column: value finite, integral for an integer column; kept is a live column other than col; ratio finite and not zero. The bounds test places the value inside the original bounds. A stack error in a primal-only result. That the absorber's bound was implied is not the structure check's to prove (a fixing is a restriction); the crush check and the oracle test it.
- A bound from a dual argument (dual_bound). Contract only: the column is live at that position, the bound is finite and integral for an integer column; a stack error in a primal-only result. It restricts, so nothing else is to prove.
- A merge. Two variables: V for the kept column before the merge and rho for the removed column, with the boxes of the record. In the reverse pass after[m] = map of the kept index (its meaning as z), then the kept index maps to V and the removed one to rho. Lifting: in a composed function, the pairs are visited in increasing position; a pair whose coefficients satisfy g_rho = scale * g_V within composed_zero is replaced by g_V on after[m]; a pair that does not lift stays as two variables with their boxes, which is sound because the split keeps both inside, and the identity V + scale * rho = after[m] is offered as one more row to a proof that also uses an anchor after the merge. Obligations: the range of after[m] over the box of its variable lies inside the merged range of the record (merge_failures); the conditions that leave no hole are recomputed from the record and the types. The bounds test compares the original columns with the boxes of V and rho, so a later fixing of z at 2 is no bound failure.

D. Three more changes of the structure check. (1) The else at :292 becomes an explicit row_addition branch, and any other kind is the stack error 'unknown record kind'. (2) New test objective: D = the original objective composed through the maps less the reduced objective. In stack order, for every defined record, D loses (D on the record's variable / a) times (its saved row less its side); then the pairs of the merges are lifted. Every coefficient of D must be zero within composed_zero of its parts and the constant within identity_tolerance of its terms. This reads the cost move of the doubleton substitution, of R2 and R5, the zero cost of R3 and R4 and the equal costs of a merge; the file reads no cost today. (3) The postsolve test (:1042-1058) takes three points of the reduced model (every column at its lower bound, at its upper bound, in the middle, an infinite bound replaced by the other one plus or minus 1, integers rounded) and replays the records forward on the postsolved point: every saved row holds at its position, every column of a record is inside its box, at a merge the two parts are inside their boxes, and the replay ends at the point it started from. This is the second implementation of the split and of the undo of R3 and R4. New verdicts in gprs_check.cpp:394-413: structure_<mode>_objective, _columns (boxes, integral forms, snapshots), _merges; the same part names in gprs_fuzz.cpp:333-339. A row may hold two line columns when they are a continuous twin pair: both continuous, proportional in every row with scale 1, -1, 2 or -2, one of them with two finite bounds, at most one huge bound in the pair, at most two pairs per model. The solver replaces the pair by the merged column t = x_p + s * x_q and takes the best of at most four cases, one per finite bound v of either twin: with x_q = v the model has the one line column t with bounds [l_p + s * v, u_p + s * v], cost c_p and the constant D * v, D = c_q - s * c_p; with x_p = v the column t has the bounds of v + s * [l_q, u_q], cost c_q / s and the constant -(D / s) * v. Each case is inside the class. What is measured. Three measures: remaining nonzeros, rows and columns, each as the geometric mean of reference / engine over the instances that have the reference; on the 240 reported over 237 (without ex9, ex10, enlight_hard) and over 240. Gate, on the 40 and on the 240.
1. Geometric mean at least 0.99 in each of the three measures. A lower bound only.
2. Every instance below 0.95 in any measure is explained, or it is a defect. Explained means: a rule that Stage 1c leaves out on purpose, shown by a HiGHS run without that rule or by an engine run; an ablation number alone is not enough for the instances that no single switch loses.
3. A second lower bound per instance: not more nonzeros, rows or columns than the Stage 1 engine of today (v4pre_reductions.csv), every exception explained; the nine instances where the engine is ahead are listed one by one.

The reference takes 302 s on the 240 against 18 s for the engine, so this rule is slack; the overheads are reported with G/analysis/overheads.py as in every run.

Today the whole engine has a median of 5.1 passes, 8.3 at the 90% quantile, 23.8 at most (script a8.py). RISKS
- The gate in rows depends on one instance. Without neos-3402294-bobin the measured single-rule losses end at 0.9874 in rows, and the chain that reaches bobin is a hypothesis until the HiGHS run of M0 and the engine's run after M1. If the chain needs a row-dual bound or the single-equation extension, a rule of the left-out set has to be built or the rows gate is missed.
- A one-rule-off run measures what HiGHS loses, not what our form gains. Weaker forms chosen here: explicit bounds only in every test (HiGHS reads implied bounds in simple probing and in bound strengthening); no dual bound from another column of the row in R5; the force test of R8 on the lock row only; exact zero cost in R3 and R4; costs equal to 1e-12 relative in R9; no single-equation extension. By measured losses the margin at the end is 0.002 without bobin and 0.006 to 0.009 with it, less than one family.
- 2.2 of the 10.6 summed log gap in nonzeros, 3.1 of 8.4 in rows and 2.2 of 14.1 in columns have no single-rule attribution. Besides bobin these are instances where rules stand in for each other (doon, neos-827175, unitcal_7, n5-3, icir97_tension, savsched1, the physiciansched instances). Whether the built rules reach them is unknown; they are the first candidates for the list below 0.95.
- The validity arguments of the exchange rules (R5 with two end tests, R6 with a weakly implied absorber bound that reads a row holding the fixed column, R7 without a lock in the other direction, R8 with one candidate binary per row, release of the locking side only and several aliases in one transaction) were derived by reading and re-derived by me; none was run. The oracle campaign of each milestone is their first test. R8's reduction to the largest binary rests on both tests being lower limits on its coefficient; for an integer column the force limit depends on the column's own coefficient, which the per-column test keeps.
- The structure-check extension of M1 (check variables, anchors, next_anchor, windows) and of M5 (merge variables, lifting, the identity) is the largest piece of check work and changes the file that the row-addition work has just stabilised (three holes were found in its arithmetic by review, HISTORY Entry 31). The same kind of review is needed again. A proof that offers an anchor is sound only if that anchor holds; the anchors of an interval record hold only if their own obligation passes, so a failure there has to be read before any other.
- The forward crush is new engine code and three checks lean on it. A crush error and a rule error look alike on a point that is not optimal; unit tests of crush alone and the wrong engines cr_replay and pd_crush are there for that. A future dual rule whose exchange moves a kept column must bring a record for it, or the default-mode crush check reports false failures.
- Fixed-point flag. It rests on the event list of the driver, which was derived by reading; I checked every push site of state_ops.cpp but ran nothing. A premise without an event shows only as a second presolve that still reduces. Four causes clear the flag on purpose (bucket limit, absorbers above the limit, the saved-row limit, a refusal by the growth factor); M1 and M3 report how often.
- Rounds. The column families act once per round and their findings reach the cascade in the next round, while max_rounds = 20 counts over the whole presolve; today the largest count is 13. Chains inside the singleton family no longer cost rounds, but an interplay such as singleton column, lock rule, redundant rows (the bobin hypothesis) costs one round per alternation.
- Time and work. Each round expands the listed rows to their columns for the dual family and tests every such column by one column scan; this is of the order of one to two propagation sweeps per round and is what the ceilings of the gate watch. The wider list masks put four more lists on the hot path of a bound change. After M1 many rows change and the parallel rows re-key them; in HiGHS that stage takes 80 s on rd-rplusc-21, and ours was not measured on such states.
- Numerics of substitutions. R1 and R8 put scale * a into the rows of the binary with only alias_scale_limit = 1e6 as a guard, and R2 and R5 move cost with only the cancellation rule. The growth factor bounds chains of R2 and R5 but does not follow a saved column through a later alias, and with the limit at 1000 a restored value can pass its bound by 1e-3 at a reduced point that uses the full tolerance; the check that solves the reduced model with HiGHS is where this shows.
- The engine is ahead of the reference on nine instances through the row addition. The new phases run after the Stage 1 phases, but from the second round on singleton columns may be gone before the parallel rows see them. The per-instance lower bound against v4pre catches this; the remedy would be an order change.
- R3 turns equations into ranged rows, which the doubleton rule and the later aggregator cannot use as pivot rows; R8 makes a full HiGHS presolve end larger on two of the seven instances tested for it (G/baseline/HIGHS_DIAG.md:828-829); a merged integer pair is no binary. All three are judged here by the cheap-rules gate and have to be looked at again when sparsify, the aggregator and the clique rules exist. Solver quality is not tested locally.
- Oracle class. R2, R3 and R5 with a second continuous column in the pivot row are tested by sampling only (planted points, real instances). The twin solver adds a case split to the checker itself; its exactness argument holds for two continuous twins of which one has two finite bounds, and the generator must not leave that.
- Sources and measurements. PaPILO was not read in this round. The attribution of losses to R7 against R8 and to dominance against merge is by counters, not by switches, and the type of a merged pair is inferred from an instance's counts of integer columns. The stored seconds come from a shared node. The work ceilings and the fixed-point number are proposals without a measurement of the new families behind them.

DECISIONS FOR THE OWNER
- 1. Leaving the low-worth rules out of the required set (dominated and weakly dominated column by the bound on the reduced cost, implied equations, column stuffing, implied-integer conversion, the single-equation extension of dual fixing). Recommended: leave them out, on one condition: the bobin run of M0. What it costs if they stay out: measured on HiGHS on all 240 instances with all of them off together, 1.0006 in nonzeros, 1.0015 in rows, 1.0006 in columns (19 instances; csched007 loses 50 of 347 rows and will stand below 0.95 in rows as an accepted exception; ns1208400 is reached by another route already). Not measured: the single-equation extension, which has no switch and stands in for the dominated test in every stored run; the engine's unexplained distance bounds it at 0.0024 in the mean log of nonzeros, and M0 measures it. The consequence beyond this stage: the engine keeps no bounds on row duals, which is the one large piece of state these rules need and which later dual rules (pairwise dominated columns, a stronger free-column substitution) would also read; leaving it out now means building it then. What it costs if the condition fails: bobin alone is 0.012 of the mean log in rows, and without it the rows gate ends at 0.9874; the one rule that reaches bobin would then move into the required set. What it costs to build them all now: row-dual bounds with their invalidation, no forward map for the crush check on two of the rules, a third column type for one, for at most 0.0015 measured.
- 2. Merging two binary or integer columns into one general integer. HiGHS does it (two binaries with equal cost become one integer in [0,2], read at H/HPresolveParallelRowsAndCols.cpp:315-323 and 397-417); PaPILO does not on pure binary models (the task text and the first design; not read by me). Three settings are built. Recommended as the default everywhere: range_kept, which merges an integer pair only when dual bound strengthening gives the merged column the range of the kept one back in the same step, so two binaries stay one binary; continuous pairs and an integer merged into a continuous column are always merged. What the recommendation costs against the gate, measured on HiGHS by instance: about 0.0006 of the mean log in nonzeros and 0.0010 in columns; neos-4722843-widden stays at 0.917 in nonzeros and 0.829 in columns as an accepted exception, fiball at 0.983; rail507, fast0507 and seymour are reached, because there HiGHS's own bound strengthening takes 807 of 828 merged columns back to [0,1]. What the setting always costs: the later clique and probing stages and cuOpt's heuristics get a column of range 2 or more where two binaries were, so it is no member of a clique, no binary to probe on, no candidate for R1, R8, GF2, indicator strengthening or block BVE; what it gives besides the gate is one column for two interchangeable ones. Neither the harm nor the benefit to the solve is measured. M5 reports the gate with both settings, and the question comes back when cliques and probing exist or with a cluster run of both. The plan's present text (A8) has the integer merge behind a switch that is off inside cuOpt; range_kept is a setting that can stay on there.
- 3. The gate in three measures. The plan now says rows and columns are measured as well as nonzeros, without thresholds. Recommended: the same two numbers on each measure (geometric mean at least 0.99, every instance below 0.95 explained), and 'explained' to mean a run that names the rule, since nine instances have no single-rule number. Consequence to know before agreeing: by measured losses the rows gate is met only if bobin is reached, and eight instances will have to be explained in columns. The alternative is nonzeros as the gate and the other two as reports, which a weak R3 or R9 would pass.
- 4. Primal-only mode for the zero-cost singleton columns (R3) and the forcing column (R4). Both are exact projections and keep the sentence of the mode (every feasible point crushes to a feasible point with the same objective); the plan's classes put them among the rules that are off there. Recommended: on. It costs one exemption in the round-trip clause of the crush check for their columns, of the kind the merge needs anyway, and gives 1.0064 in columns for R3 in that mode (measured on HiGHS). Until you decide they are off.
- 5. The place of the single-lock alias (R8). It is in the required set by A8 and is also the port of cuOpt's single-lock rule (A6). Its separable worth is 0.0012 of the mean log in nonzeros on 18 instances, and in a full HiGHS presolve two of seven tested instances end larger with it. Recommended: keep it in the required set, built as the second step of M4 after R7 has been measured alone, with its switch kept so that it can be turned off when the aggregator stage is measured. The alternative is to move it to the optional milestone, which leaves var-smallemery-m6j6 (0.86) and neos859080 (0.94) below 0.95 as accepted exceptions.
- 6. Work ceilings and rounds. Proposed next to your time rule, which is slack on every instance: the four new families together at most 2 passes of the input model in the median and 6 at the 90% quantile, no instance on max_rounds = 20, at most 5 instances losing the fixed-point flag. These are numbers without a measurement behind them; say if you want other ones, or want them as reports only. Raising max_rounds is a default change that needs your word if an instance ends on it.
