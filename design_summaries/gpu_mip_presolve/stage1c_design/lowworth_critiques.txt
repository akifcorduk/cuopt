What holds. In exact arithmetic I found no model on which a stated condition of the five rules holds and the reduction loses the optimum, makes a feasible model infeasible or gives a wrong postsolved point. The reading of HiGHS is right: original row-dual bounds are 0 or infinite (H/HPresolve.cpp:146-149), an implied bound is cost over coefficient of one non-integer column with at most one infinite term in its original sum (:657-725, :3629-3631), and that count is our lock count (G/src/state_ops.cpp:88-99). The circular cases are harmless as designed: a locked compensator sits in a row with two finite sides, that row holds no second moving column (it locks every column both ways, so C1 fails for any other), and its activity does not change. The single-equation marks equal checkColumn (H/HPresolveDualFixing.cpp:110-131, 158-229), the two stuffing tests equal H/HPresolveColStuffing.cpp:193-224 with the weaker second test proved by the exchange, and the weak implied-integer test needs no 'no equation' clause. Primal-only mode keeps what it must in exact arithmetic: the exchange family and the weak test are off, the equation test and the scaling are true at every feasible point. The figures I spot-checked agree with the design (huahum 19,658 against 19,664 columns in off_rule17.csv and unchanged in off_leftout.csv; b1c1s1; csched007 347 rows; supportcase42 18,439; physiciansched3-3 +209 and +36 rows; sp97ar).

What is wrong. Eight defects, all outside the exact proofs: four serious (the tolerance of the weak implied test, which loses an optimum with the bound kept next to the new equation; rule 5 run on marks read before rule 2 converted the row in the same scan; the integrality predicate of rule 4 without the drift guard the engine's own row tightening has; the undo of the nine-rule records reading input types after a conversion) and four minor (bound rounding at a conversion, crush of a converted column, the composition rule of the column scaling, fixed cases that never reach their rule). The first and the third are not seen by the oracle as designed, because its data are integers and powers of two (G/tests/oracle.hpp:76-106). The justification given for decision 2 ('weak is enough because the bound stays in the model') is wrong as stated and has to change before milestone L1.
