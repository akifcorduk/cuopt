--- R1. Simple probing on an equation (family simple_probing; milestone M1)
Condition: Row r of row_list::simple_probing is live, row_lower == row_upper == b finite with |b| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility, and row_free_size[r] >= 3 (two free entries are a doubleton equation and stay with doubleton_t). After refresh_activity neither activity has an infinite term (min_act.inf_count[r] == 0 and max_act.inf_count[r] == 0), so every free column has finite bounds. Quick test in O(1): |(M - b) - (b - m)| less both error bounds is at most tol.feasibility, m and M being the compensated minimum and maximum activity. Then one scan of the row looks for the binary p: a free integer column with col_upper - col_lower == 1 exactly for which both conditional rows are forcing in the engine's own sense: gap_low = (b - m) - |a_p| and gap_high = (M - b) - |a_p| each pass the test of forcing_row (E/src/rules_rows.cpp:152-180) evaluated on the free entries other than p: |gap| + error <= tol.feasibility * min|a_i| / max(1, max|a_i|), and when one of those columns is continuous the gap may be positive only by rounding (the error bound plus forcing_rounding_units rounding units of the terms). Smallest column index among several (in exact arithmetic at most one column can qualify when the row has three or more free entries, because the ranges of the other columns add up to |a_p|). Every other free column i has u_i - l_i <= alias_scale_limit = 1e6 (a named constant and a function of the state, so a second presolve skips the same rows). Effect: delete_row(r); every other free column i gets x_i = v_low_i + (v_high_i - v_low_i) * (x_p - l_p), where v_high_i is the bound the row forces on i when x_p = l_p + 1 (for a_p > 0 the minimum-activity bound of i: l_i if a_i > 0, else u_i) and v_low_i its other bound; a_p < 0 exchanges the two. The bounds are read before the first substitution. Applied in full with the substitution code shared with the doubleton family, with no budget test inside the row and no bound transfer (the image of the two values of p is exactly the two bounds of i).
Validity: Class B/S, primal: the alias holds at every feasible point; on in primal-only mode. Proof: the term of p takes one of two values that differ by |a_p|. With the larger one the rest of the row must equal its minimum activity plus gap_low, with the smaller one its maximum activity minus gap_high; both gaps are zero within the forcing tolerance, so each other column sits at the bound named above. Floating point: this is the conclusion the engine already draws for a forcing row, with the same tolerance and the same restriction to rounding-size gaps when a continuous column is in the row (a real gap g leaves such a column g/|a_i| off its bound, and another row that holds it with a larger coefficient would be violated by more; rules_rows.cpp:140-151). I take this test and not the absolute 1e-9 of HiGHS (H/presolve/HPresolve.cpp:2696-2698, read) that worth_first and engine_fit copied: an absolute gap of 1e-9 is not small for a column with |a_i| = 1e-6. An integer column gets an integral scale and offset because its bounds are integral, which the stack contract asks (E/include/gprs/postsolve.hpp:77-81). Activities are over explicit bounds only (HiGHS also reads implied bounds); a row that balances only through an implied bound of a continuous column fires in the rounds after make_explicit.
Postsolve: Existing alias record with the new field origin = probing and row = r. Undo x_i = scale * x_p + offset as today. Crush: nothing; p stays and every feasible point satisfies the alias. The deleted equation leaves no record: with the aliases composed it is a constant, which the rows test of the structure check covers as it is.
Cost: O(1) per listed row for the quick test, where most rows end; one row scan for the binary; then the live entries of the substituted columns and of p, spent on the family's budget {4, 2, 1e5}. The stop is tested between two rows; a row whose columns do not fit the remaining budget goes back on the list. A column is substituted once, so the total over the presolve is at most the nonzeros. Called once per round, after the doubleton equations. The list gets every activity, activity_unread, side and entries event of a row, so nothing is left to history and the family never reports limited.
Worth: Measured (G/results/bench240/cheap_ablation/off_simple_probing.csv, recomputed in abl.out): without it HiGHS keeps 1.0103 times the nonzeros, 1.0106 the rows and 1.0220 the columns in the geometric mean over 237 instances; 8 instances change: highschool1-aigio 3.04, neos-1445765 1.96, brazil3 1.86, the two cryptanalysis instances 1.009, academictimetablesmall 1.007. Counters (reader): 296,239 substitutions on 13 instances, 98% on the first three. Predicted gate after it: about 0.968 (0.9563 today).

--- R2. Free singleton column with an equation as pivot row (family column_rules; M2)
Condition: Column k is live with csc.size[k] == 1; its entry a lies in the live row r with row_lower == row_upper == b finite and row_free_size[r] >= 2. b' = b less the terms of the fixed columns, |b'| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility. Implied free by r alone on explicit bounds, in O(1) from the stored activities: with rest_min and rest_max the activity of r without the term of k, the ends low = (b' - rest_max)/a and high = (b' - rest_min)/a (exchanged for a < 0), each moved outward by candidate_slack (E/src/rules_propagation.cpp:140-152), must satisfy: col_lower[k] == -inf or low >= col_lower[k] - implied_tolerance, and col_upper[k] == +inf or high <= col_upper[k] + implied_tolerance, with implied_tolerance(bound) = 2 * (4 * rounding_unit * |bound| + tol.activity_error_share * tol.feasibility), which is what two candidates of one bound can differ by (the lower limit of improvement_step, rules_propagation.cpp:57-67). An end whose residual has an infinite term fails unless the bound of k on that side is infinite. The error guard of bound propagation applies, with one fresh summation when it drops an end. Integer k: every other free column of r is integer, every a_i/a is integral within tol.zero with a * round(a_i/a) equal to a_i within tol.zero, and b'/a is integral within tol.zero with |a * round(b'/a) - b'| <= tol.feasibility (the test the alias forest makes for an edge between integers, postsolve.hpp:160-165); otherwise the column is left and nothing is concluded. With a finite bound of k: max|a_i| / |a| <= pivot_ratio_limit = 1000 (named constant). q = cost[k]/a and every new cost are finite. Effect as one transaction: save the row; delete_row(r); set_cost(i, cost_i - q * a_i) for every saved entry with the cancellation rule of substitute() (rules_doubleton.cpp:148-157); add_objective_offset(q, b'); set_cost(k, 0); remove_col(k). No row gains an entry.
Validity: Class S, primal; on in primal-only mode. At every feasible point x_k = (b' - g)/a, g being the saved entries, so the cost moves exactly and crush is dropping k with equal objective. The end test is the range of (b' - g)/a over the explicit bounds of the other columns, so the restored value is inside the bounds of k (to the tolerance) for every point inside the bounds of the reduced model, not only for feasible ones; k is in no other row, so the row carries nothing else. One implied premise, the pivot row itself, recomputed at application. No recorded implied bound is read: a record depends on the order of visits and on the 1e-3 step (rules_propagation.cpp:168-188) and a second presolve builds it differently. After make_explicit the explicit bound of a singleton column is the candidate of its only row moved outward, so the test still passes and a second presolve decides the same. The test of b'/a for an integer singleton, which PaPILO's SingletonCols lacks by the reader's report, is part of the condition; 2x + 2y = 3 with an integer singleton x is a fixed oracle case.
Postsolve: New record col_from_rows {col, n_rows = 1, first_row, col_lower = -inf, col_upper = +inf, prefer = 0} with one saved row {row r, coefficient a, lower = upper = b', the free entries of r other than k}. Fixed terms are folded into b' as read_edge does (rules_doubleton.cpp:42-68), so a saved row names only columns that are live at the push. Undo: x_k = (b' - compensated sum of the saved entries at the restored values)/a, rounded to the nearest integer for an integer column; not clamped (the row stays exact, the bound holds to the tolerance). Crush: k is dropped.
Cost: O(1) to accept or reject on the stored activities; a firing costs the row once (save and cost move), for an integer column one more scan. The objective gains up to len(r) - 1 nonzero costs, reported as the statistic cost_fill. Saved entries count against options_t::saved_row_factor (4) times the input nonzeros; at that limit the family reports limited.
Worth: Measured for HiGHS rule 8, equation and inequality pivots together (off_rule8.csv): 1.0089 nonzeros, 1.0026 rows, 44 instances: mcsched 1.63, swath1 and swath3 1.245, neos-3627168-kasai 1.226, neos-3046615-murg 1.127, blp-ar98 1.098, irish-electricity 1.090, supportcase7 1.071, leo1 1.063. Counters (reader): 16,881 firings on 59 instances, the most widespread cheap rule; one firing removes a whole row. The counters do not split the two pivot kinds; M2 measures R5 by its own switch.

--- R3. Zero-cost singleton column (family column_rules; M2)
Condition: Column k is live, continuous, csc.size[k] == 1, cost[k] == 0 exactly; its row r has two finite sides (an equation included) and row_free_size[r] >= 2; R2 did not take it. With a the coefficient: L' = L - max(a*l_k, a*u_k) and U' = U - min(a*l_k, a*u_k), a new side being infinite where the bound it needs is infinite. Skipped when a needed finite bound gives an infinite term (is_infinite_term) or a finite new side v has |v| * tol.side_error > tol.feasibility. Effect: save the row; delete_entry of (r, k); set_row_lower(L') and set_row_upper(U'), or delete_row when both are infinite; remove_col(k). A one-sided row is not this rule's: there the column has no lock in one direction, and the lock rule (finite bound) or R4 (infinite bound) owns it, as in HiGHS (H/presolve/HPresolveZeroCostSingleton.cpp:15-31, read). Not taken for an integer column (the set of rest values would have holes).
Validity: Exact projection: the values g of the other columns for which some x_k in [l_k, u_k] satisfies L <= a*x_k + g <= U are exactly L' <= g <= U' (the column is continuous, so the union of the shifted intervals is one interval). The cost is zero, so every feasible point crushes by dropping k with the same objective, and every reduced point extends. No lock, activity or optimality argument is used, so no error bound enters; each new side is one compensated difference (shifted_side, E/src/state_ops.cpp:155-161) under the side guard. Mode: the plan puts singleton-column rules in class D and switches D off in primal-only mode (PLAN/DESIGN.md:437, 630-632), although the rule keeps the sentence of options_t::primal_only (E/include/gprs/presolve.hpp:83-85). It is off in primal-only mode by one test inside the family until the owner decides (decision 2).
Postsolve: col_from_rows {col k, one saved row with the original sides L and U less the fixed terms and the free entries other than k, col_lower = l_k, col_upper = u_k, prefer = l_k if finite, else u_k if finite, else 0}. Undo: the interval that the bounds and the saved row allow for x_k at the restored values; x_k is its point nearest to prefer; when rounding makes it empty, the point of [l_k, u_k] nearest to the row's interval. Deterministic, no search. Crush: k is dropped.
Cost: O(1) test; a firing costs the row once. The row stays in the model, so repeated removals from one long row are what saved_row_factor bounds.
Worth: Measured (off_rule18.csv): 1.0012 nonzeros and 1.0064 columns, 16 instances: timtab1 1.098, neos-4954672-berkel 1.086, icir97_tension 1.055 (columns 1.77), pg 1.020, supportcase12 1.012. Counters: 58,511 firings on 19 instances, 55,681 on supportcase12. It is one of the rules on the path to neos-3402294-bobin (132 firings there; see R4).

--- R4. Forcing column (family column_rules; M2)
Condition: Column k is live, of any type and length, cost[k] == 0 exactly, and either down_locks[k] == 0 with col_lower[k] == -inf and up_locks[k] >= 1, or the mirror. This is exactly the case the lock rule leaves today (E/src/rules_dual_fixing.cpp:49-55; rules.hpp:309-310 says 'Stage 3 removes it with its rows'). Effect: every live row of k is saved without k and deleted, then remove_col(k). When the used slots of its rows do not fit the remaining budget the column goes back on the list.
Validity: Lock argument with zero cost; an exact projection. No down lock means that every row of k is one-sided in the way that lowering x_k never hurts (a ranged row or an equation would lock both ways), and the bound below is infinite: for any values of the other columns a value of x_k satisfies all its rows and its upper bound, so these rows restrict nothing else, and the objective does not see x_k. Lock counts do not depend on bounds or activities (state_ops.cpp:88-99), so no tolerance enters the premise; the cost test is exact zero as in the lock rule (HiGHS allows a small tolerance by the reader's report). An integer column is rounded further into the free direction, which keeps every saved row satisfied. Class L by the plan: off in primal-only mode until decision 2.
Postsolve: col_from_rows with all rows of k (each with its coefficient, its sides less the fixed terms and its free entries other than k), col_lower, col_upper, prefer = the finite bound, or 0 when both are infinite. Undo: the interval that all saved rows and the bounds allow, its finite end rounded inward for an integer column, then the point nearest to prefer; in the down-free case this is min(u_k, min over the rows of (side - g_i)/a_i), floored for an integer. Crush: k is dropped.
Cost: O(1) test; a firing costs the entries of the rows of k once. Those rows leave the model, so the saved entries of this rule are bounded by the nonzeros.
Worth: Measured (off_rule7.csv): 1.0001 nonzeros and 1.0005 rows on three instances (neos-2746589-doon 1.027 in nonzeros and 1.124 in rows, neos-3656078-kumeu 1.006, neos-3402454-bohle in columns only). Counters: 381 columns and 2,856 rows on 5 instances. neos-3402294-bobin, the largest gain of the set (ratio 0.19; 0.0070 of the 0.0447 mean log gap) has the same 381,000 nonzeros in all thirteen ablation files. Its all-cheap run shows (bobin.py, from highs_cheap_all/summary.json) 132 forcing columns that remove 132 rows, so they are singleton columns, 132 zero-cost singletons, then 1,848 fixings counted as weakly dominated and 556,248 redundant rows, while the lock rule fixes nothing on the input. The chain is therefore R3 and R4, then the lock rule the engine has. That it carries over to the engine is inferred, not run; M2 tests it.

--- R5. Free singleton column with an inequality as pivot row (family column_rules; M2; the first new dual rule)
Condition: Not in primal-only mode. Column k is live with csc.size[k] == 1 and cost[k] != 0; its row r is no equation. The tight side S is the side of r that k is pushed against when it moves to lower cost: the activity then moves in the direction -sign(cost) * sign(a); downward takes row_lower, upward row_upper; S must be finite (when it is infinite the column has no lock in its cost direction and the lock rule owns it). Then every test of R2 with b' = S less the fixed terms: both ends of (S' - rest)/a against the bounds of k within implied_tolerance (an infinite bound needs nothing), the integrality conditions for an integer k, the side guard and pivot_ratio_limit. Effect as R2: the row is deleted and the cost moves onto its other columns.
Validity: Class D, an exchange that moves only the removed column; off in primal-only mode. From a feasible point move x_k in its cost direction until r is tight at S: x_k' = (S' - g)/a. No other row holds k; the bound in the direction of the move is implied by the end test; the bound behind it is not passed because the move goes away from it; the cost falls by |cost| times the distance; for an integer k the value is integral by the conditions. So an optimal point has r tight, and the reduced model is the original restricted to that. The second end test is not needed for this exchange; it makes the restored value keep the far bound for every point of the reduced model. validity_first and worth_first asked for an infinite far bound instead; that is the special case, and engine_fit's two-end form is the one taken. It is the plan's pivot row 'weakly dual-implied with the pivot column as source' (PLAN/DESIGN.md:469-470) and the case HiGHS reaches in the cheap setting (H/presolve/HPresolve.cpp:2457-2485, read; that the implied dual bound of the row is cost/a is the reader's inference). A row whose dual sign is known only through another column is not taken, because no row-dual state is built.
Postsolve: col_from_rows as in R2 with lower = upper = S'. Undo x_k = (S' - sum)/a. Crush: k is dropped; the reduced objective of the crushed point is the objective of the moved point, so it is not above that of the point. The finding counts in dual_findings.
Cost: As R2.
Worth: Part of the 1.0089 of rule 8; M2 measures its share by switching it alone. With R2 to R4 this is the largest milestone in the prediction: gate about 0.968 -> 0.985, of which bobin is 0.007.

--- R6. Parallel columns: fixing by dominance (family parallel_cols; M3)
Condition: Not in primal-only mode. Detection: per live column a key (number of live entries; hash over row and coefficient divided by the coefficient in the smallest row index), recomputed for columns whose entries changed. A bucket is the set of columns with equal key in index order. A singleton column has its row as key, so all singleton columns of one row form one bucket, as in HiGHS (H/presolve/HPresolveParallelRowsAndCols.cpp:38-40, 75-76, read). Exact test of a pair p < q: the same live rows and one ratio s = a_q/a_p with |a_q - s*a_p| <= tol.zero * |a_q| in every row and |a_q - s*a_p| * R <= tol.activity_error_share * tol.feasibility, R being the largest finite |bound| of the column that is fixed (tol.feasibility/tol.side_error when one is infinite). D = cost_q - s * cost_p as a compensated value; the costs count as equal when |D| <= tol.cancellation * (|cost_q| + |s * cost_p|). Moving delta from q to p (x_q - delta, x_p + s * delta) changes no row and changes the cost by -delta * D. Cases in this order: (a) D >= 0 or equal: q is fixed at its finite lower bound when p can absorb s * (x_q - l_q); (b) D <= 0 or equal: q is fixed at its finite upper bound when p can absorb in the other direction; (c) and (d): the same with the roles exchanged (ratio 1/s). 'Can absorb in a direction': the bound of the absorber in that direction is infinite, or one of its rows implies it: the candidate of that row over the explicit bounds of its other columns (O(1) from the stored activities, moved outward by candidate_slack) is within implied_tolerance of the bound; the rows of the absorber are scanned and the first that implies is taken. Types: an integer absorber needs an integer moved column and an integral factor (s or 1/s within tol.zero); a continuous absorber takes a moved column of either type; a continuous column is never moved onto an integer. An infinite target bound is left alone (no status is concluded). One pair at a time, every premise computed at application. A bucket of singleton columns is scanned as a group when a member is listed: one pass finds the absorbers (the implied test of a singleton column reads only its row); without an absorber nothing more is done and nothing is left undone; with absorbers every other member is tested against them in index order, at most opt.parallel_bucket_limit absorbers per member, beyond which the call reports limited.
Validity: Class D, an exchange with a recorded compensation; off in primal-only mode. For a feasible point move the fixed column to its bound and the absorber by the factor. Every row keeps its activity. The absorber stays inside its bound: the bound is infinite, or it follows from one row and the explicit bounds of that row's other columns, and after the move that row still holds and every other column, the fixed one included, is inside its explicit bounds. One implied premise, tested weakly, which is enough because the bound stays in the model and nothing is dropped on its strength. The cost does not rise by the sign of D; integrality holds by the type rule. HiGHS has the same four cases with the weak implied test for a MIP (HPresolveParallelRowsAndCols.cpp:143-157, 249-286, read) and accepts |D| up to its dual tolerance; here the sign of D is exact apart from the cancellation tolerance, so that 'not above' in the checks needs no exception. Rejected: engine_fit's form with a plain fixed-column record and crush promising nothing, which no solver-free check could test where the rule fires; and engine_fit's exclusion of singleton columns (PaPILO skips them, ParallelColDetection.hpp:204-205, read on the node): on neos-3004026-krka, satellites2-60-fs and satellites2-40 every column this rule fixes in HiGHS is a singleton (6,435, 820 and 600 fixings with as many entries) and on rail01 and rail02 most are (par.out).
Postsolve: New record parallel_fix {col, value, kept, ratio}, written by a helper next to fix_column (E/src/rules_bounds.cpp). Undo: x[col] = value. Crush, at the record's position in the replay: w[kept] += ratio * (w[col] - value), then col is dropped. The record exists so that the default-mode crush check and the oracle test this rule on feasible points: a wrong implied test puts the absorber outside its bound, a wrong sign raises the objective.
Cost: Keys: the entries of the columns whose entries changed. Comparisons: a listed column against at most opt.parallel_bucket_limit neighbours above and below in its bucket, each the column length; a scan that ends on the limit with columns left reports limited, as the parallel rows do (E/include/gprs/rules.hpp:228-233). Absorb test: one scan of the absorber with O(1) per row. A parallel pair that is left alone marks both columns; only while marks exist does the family expand its row list to the marked columns of the listed rows, otherwise it drops that list. Budget {8, 8, 1e5}, once per round. HiGHS's column loop costs 2.3 s on the 240 instances (reader).

--- R7. Dual bound strengthening (family column_rules; M4)
Condition: Not in primal-only mode. Column j is live with down_locks >= 1 and up_locks >= 1 (a column without a lock in one direction is the lock rule's, which stays first and unchanged in the cascade). Direction down when cost >= 0 (a new upper bound), up when cost <= 0; with cost == 0 down first and up only when down changed nothing. Down: for every live entry (i, a) of j that locks it down (a > 0 with finite row_lower, a < 0 with finite row_upper), w_i = (side - rest)/a, rest being the activity of row i without the term of j at the end that works against that side (the minimum for the lower side, the maximum for the upper side), O(1) from the stored activities. The attempt ends without a result at the first row whose rest has an infinite term, whose side fails the side guard, or whose error bound fails the guard of bound propagation after one fresh summation. w_i is moved upward by candidate_slack; for an integer j it becomes ceil(w_i - tol.feasibility / max(1, |a|)). u' = max(col_lower, max_i w_i). Taken when u' < col_upper - tol.feasibility and |u'| * tol.side_error <= tol.feasibility. u' == col_lower: fix_column(j, col_lower). Otherwise an integer j gets tighten_upper(u') and a dual_bound record; a continuous j is fixed only when every w_i <= col_lower + tol.feasibility / max(1, |a|) and is otherwise left, so that explicit bounds of continuous columns stay what section 6 of the plan says they are. Up is the mirror.
Validity: Class D, an exchange that moves only column j; off in primal-only mode. For a feasible point with x_j > u' set x_j = u'. Every row that locks j down holds at u' whatever the other columns do inside their explicit bounds, by the definition of w_i; every other row only gains from a decrease; the cost does not rise; an integer stays integral. An equation among the rows gives a w_i that is at least the bound the equation implies, so the clip never acts through it. The activities are the engine's compensated sums with their error bound, every rounding goes to the safe side (a larger bound), and the integer rounding divides the tolerance by |a| as HiGHS does (the readers cite H/presolve/HPresolve.cpp:4442-4454), so the row is violated by at most the tolerance at the rounded bound; PaPILO's absolute 1e-9 on plain sums is not copied. Explicit bounds only; HiGHS reads implied bounds here, which the rounds after make_explicit give. The plan's guard 'dual arguments use continuous columns only' concerns columns that supply row-dual bounds; this rule uses none.
Postsolve: A fixing leaves the existing fixed_col record (crush drops the column, which is the move). A tightening leaves the new record dual_bound {col, bound, is_upper}: the primal postsolve ignores it, and crush clips w[col] to the bound at that position of the replay.

--- R8. Alias of a single-lock column to a binary of its lock row (family column_rules; M4). HiGHS's dual-fixing substitution and the port of cuOpt's single_lock_dual_aggregation in one rule
Condition: Not in primal-only mode. --- R9. Parallel columns: merge (family parallel_cols; M5)
Condition: A parallel pair as in R6 whose costs are equal (|D| within the cancellation tolerance, in both modes) and to which no case of R6 applies; in primal-only mode R6 is off and the pair comes straight here. sigma = a_removed / a_kept. ===== CHECKS =====
Principle (from validity_first): every built rule has a forward map. For the primal rules crush keeps the point; for the dual rules the replay performs the exchange (R6 by its record, R7 by the clip, R5 and R8 and every dual fixing by dropping the column). So for any feasible point x of the original model the crushed point is feasible in the reduced model and its objective is not above that of x, in default mode too, and equal when result.dual_findings == 0. With the postsolve contract this proves that the optimum is kept, and both halves can be tested without a solver on any known point. Each check extension arrives in the same milestone as the rule it reads, with wrong engines in check/make_mutants.py that the named check must catch; a wrong engine that is not caught stops the milestone.

===== RISKS =====
- The ablation measures what HiGHS loses without a rule, not what our form of the rule gains. Weaker forms chosen here: explicit bounds only in every test (HiGHS reads implied bounds in simple probing and in bound strengthening); no dual bound from another column of the row in R5; the force test of R8 on the lock row only; exact zero cost in R3 and R4; costs equal to 1e-12 relative in R9; no single-equation extension. The margin after M5 is about 0.009 in the mean log, the size of one family.
- neos-3402294-bobin is 0.0070 of the 0.0447 mean log gap and no single switch loses it. The chain (132 singleton forcing columns, 132 zero-cost singletons, then the lock rule) is read from HiGHS's counters; it was not run in the engine. If the engine misses it after M2 the gate margin is nearly used up by this one instance, which is why M2 stops there.
- The validity arguments of the exchange rules (R5 with two end tests, R6 with a weakly implied absorber bound that reads a row holding the fixed column, R7, R8 with release of the locking side only) were derived by the designers and re-derived by me by reading; none was run. The oracle campaign of each milestone is their first test. R5's two-end form and R8's locking-side release go beyond what HiGHS and cuOpt do.
- The forward crush is new engine code and three checks lean on it. A crush error and a rule error look alike on a point that is not optimal; unit tests of crush alone and the wrong engines cr_replay and pd_crush are there for that. A future dual rule whose exchange moves a kept column must bring a record for it, as parallel_fix does, or the default-mode crush check reports false failures.
- The structure-check extension of M2 (linear forms, virtual columns, snapshot chain) and of M5 (merge with a parameter) is the largest single piece of check work and is built on an extension for row_addition records that is not merged yet (HISTORY.md:1530-1541). If it slips, a rule would arrive with a record the check cannot read, which is the situation of Entry 25. merge_unproven is a count the check cannot turn into a verdict; a wrong merge of columns that are not proportional has to be caught by the oracle and the crush check.
- Fixed-point flag. It now rests on the event list of section 7 of the driver, which was derived by reading. A premise without an event shows only as a second presolve that still reduces; the second-presolve tests, the sample in the campaign and the two event mutants are the safeguard. Long buckets of non-singleton parallel columns and singleton buckets with more absorbers than the limit clear the flag on real instances; M3 reports how many.
- Rounds. The column families act once per round and their findings reach the cascade in the next round, while max_rounds = 20 counts over the whole presolve. Chains such as forcing column, lock rule, redundant rows (bobin) may need more rounds than today. M2 reports result.rounds; raising the default costs time and needs the owner's word.
- Time. Each round expands the listed rows to their columns for column_rules, and for parallel_cols while a mark exists, and tests every listed column with locks both ways by one column scan; this is of the order of one or two propagation sweeps per round. The instances where HiGHS's extensions are slow (rd-rplusc-21 with 622 columns of 1,370 entries, neos-3402454-bohle, neos-4647030-tutaki) are the ones to watch. After M2 many columns become singletons and many rows change, and the parallel rows re-key every row whose core changed; in HiGHS that stage takes 80 s on rd-rplusc-21. Our parallel rows have a bucket limit and a budget but were not measured on such states.
- Memory. One saved row per removed singleton column: supportcase12 has 55,681 zero-cost singletons. A long row with many such columns is bounded only by saved_row_factor, which ends the rule with limited.
- The engine is ahead of the reference on nine instances through the row addition. The new phases run after the Stage 1 phases, but from the second round on singleton columns may be gone before the parallel rows see them. The per-instance lower bound against v4pre catches this; the remedy would be an order change, not a rule change.
- Numerics of substitutions. R1 and R8 put scale * a into the rows of the binary with only alias_scale_limit = 1e6 as a guard, and R2 and R5 move cost with only the cancellation rule; HISTORY.md:1513-1514 records a coefficient of -3e-9 left by a doubleton substitution. cuOpt guards its rule with a factor 1e6 per row at the cost of a scan of every row. If the campaign shows such rows, the dynamism veto of the parallel rows belongs into the shared substitution code. The implied tests allow about 2e-7, and with a pivot ratio up to 1000 a restored singleton value can pass its bound by more than 1e-6 at a reduced point that itself uses the tolerance.
- R3 turns equations into ranged rows, which the doubleton rule and the later aggregator cannot use as pivot rows; R9 turns two binaries into an integer of range 2; HIGHS_DIAG.md:828 records that the dual-fixing substitution makes a full HiGHS presolve end larger on var-smallemery-m6j6. All three are right for the cheap-rules gate and have to be looked at again when sparsify, the aggregator and the clique rules exist. Solver quality is not tested locally.
- Oracle class. R2, R3 and R5 with a second continuous column in the pivot row, and rules on rows with several unrelated continuous columns, are tested by sampling only (planted points, real instances), not exactly. The twin split adds an implementation of a case split to the checker itself.
- Sources. I read PaPILO only in ParallelColDetection.hpp (two places, on the node); every other PaPILO statement repeats the reader. HiGHS line numbers that I did not open myself are marked as the readers' in the rule entries. The stored seconds come from a shared node.

===== DECISIONS FOR OWNER =====
- Rule list. Amendment A8 names dominated and weakly dominated columns by the bound on the reduced cost, implied equations, column stuffing, implied-integer conversion and the dual-fixing extensions as a whole for Stage 1c. All three designers and I leave the first four and the single-equation part of the fifth out of the required set: together they are about 1.0007 in HiGHS's own ablation, none has a forward map the crush check can test, and without them the engine needs no row-dual state. They are the optional milestone M6, built only on evidence. This changes A8 and needs your yes. Recommended: accept.
- Primal-only mode for the zero-cost singleton (R3) and the forcing column (R4). Both are exact projections and keep the sentence of options_t::primal_only (every feasible point crushes to a feasible point with the same objective); the plan (DESIGN.md sections 4 and 8) puts them in the classes that are off there. I keep them off, as the plan says. Two designers would switch them on; that is one test per rule plus an exception in the round-trip clause of the crush check (z equals x) for their columns, the same kind of exception the merge needs for its pairs anyway. Worth in that mode: columns 1.0064 for R3.
