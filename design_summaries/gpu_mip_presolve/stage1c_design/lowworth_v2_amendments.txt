STAGE 1c, THE FIVE FURTHER RULES, VERSION 2: BINDING AMENDMENTS (2026-11-27)

Read together with lowworth_v2_design.txt (the design: rules A implied equation, B implied
integer by the equation and the weak test, C column scaling, S column stuffing, E
single-equation extension of dual fixing, D dominated and weakly dominated column; milestones
L0 to L5), lowworth_v2_answers.txt and lowworth_v2_reviews.txt (the two reviews of version 2,
with a model and a fix for every open point). Both reviewers say implementation can start from
version 2 once R1 to R9 are built. Where this file and the design disagree, this file holds.

A. Fixes from the validity review, in the order of its list
 1. (serious; before integral_for is written in M1, binding for L2) The drift guard is measured
    in the units of the candidate column: delta on the row divided by its coefficient, and
    drift <= coefficient_rounding_share * tol.feasibility * min(1, |a|). Row units stay enough
    where the column leaves the model (R2, R5, the integer source of rule A).
 2. (before L2's acceptance run) The implied_integer record stores the column's two bounds at
    the conversion, and the weak crush intersects its interval with them before it rounds.
 3. (L1, L3, L4) The rounding-only allowances get the absolute cap of the forcing row:
    |a_j| times the excess allowed is at most tol.feasibility * min|a_k| / max(1, max|a_k|).
 4. (L2) Rule C: the dry run of integral_for uses exactly the numbers scale_col will store.
 5. (L1) Every fixed case is written with all costs, bounds and types.
 6. (L5) Rule D fixes with a negative rate only when the other bound is finite and the rise is
    within the identity tolerance of the checks; the planted case has costs that differ in the
    13th digit.

B. Fixes from the worth, cost and state review
 1. (serious; before L2) The weak test has a premise over all rows of its column, so
    implied_integer_t gets a column list fed by lock events, read for live continuous columns;
    with a fixed-point test and a wrong engine that drops the feed.
 2. (serious; before L1) Rule A runs its O(1) implied test before the integrality proof of an
    integer source; one proof per row, coefficient size and call.
 3. (serious; before L3 to L5) Worth attributions corrected by the runs that arrived after the
    design: the single-equation part is measured (it changes huahum, icir97_tension and, after
    rule D, b1c1s1; nothing on the 240 in the geometric mean); seymour1 is an instance of rule
    A and of L5, not of stuffing; csched007 and roi5alpha10n8 are reached by R5 of the nine
    rules.
 4. (serious; before L2 and L4) The two families are seeded with the continuous columns only
    and allocate their row arrays at the first call that stores something.
 5. (L4, L5) Until L5 a marked row is scheduled only if it is an equation; a fixing clears a
    cached scan only when it touches a cached compensator.
 6. Order: kept as designed (A, B with C, S, E, D), which is the order of build cost; the gate
    does not depend on it. By worth D would follow B.
 7. (L0) The unknown list and the L0 runs are updated: L0 keeps the counters by source type,
    the traces of supportcase42, seymour1 and physiciansched3-3.
 8. Both families are inactive when all their sub-rules are off.

C. What the stored runs say these rules are worth (HiGHS, all cheap rules over the nine rules)
240 benchmark instances (237): 0.9994 in nonzeros, 0.9985 in rows, 0.9993 in columns; 21
instances differ (ns1208400, which the engine already reaches; kumeu; doon and bohle through
the forcing column, which is R4 of the nine; csched007 in rows, reached by R5; snowy; map10;
map16715-04; seymour1). 40 testbed instances: 0.9974 / 0.9998 / 0.9972; two instances differ
(ns1208400, supportcase42). No instance needs one of the five rules for the 0.95 bound.
