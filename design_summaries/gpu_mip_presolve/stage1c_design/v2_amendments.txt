STAGE 1c DESIGN, VERSION 2: BINDING AMENDMENTS (2026-11-27)

Read together with v2_design.txt (the design), v2_answers.txt (what version 2 changed against
the first design and why) and v2_reviews.txt (the two reviews of version 2, with a why and a
fix for every open point). Where this file and v2_design.txt disagree, this file holds.

A. Scope (owner's decision, HISTORY Entry 38: "Build them too")
1. All of HiGHS's cheap rules are required. The five that version 2 calls optional (dominated
   and weakly dominated column by the bound on the reduced cost, implied equations, column
   stuffing, implied-integer conversion, the single-equation extension of dual fixing) are built
   after R1 to R9, in the order of worth. They have their own design (the low-worth round,
   lowworth_*.txt in this directory once it is back). Decision 1 of version 2 is withdrawn and
   milestone M6 is not optional.
2. "No dual state" in State and driver item 1 holds for R1 to R9 only: bounds on the row duals
   arrive with the five rules. The groundwork of M0 must leave room for them: more families and
   rule indices than the four of R1 to R9, more record kinds, more list and event bits, a third
   column type later. Do not build any of it now; do not close the door either (no enum or mask
   that is full after the four families).
3. The Stage 1c gate (0.99 in nonzeros, rows and columns against HiGHS with all cheap rules on,
   on the 40 testbed and the 240 benchmark instances) is measured after the five rules. The run
   at the end of M5 is a measurement of the nine rules.
4. Order of the milestones as in version 2: singleton columns (M1) before simple probing (M2),
   the merge (M5) after the dual rules (M4).

B. The bobin test of M0 (measured, HiGHS diag2, presolve only, 2 instances)
With the Stage 1 switch set and presolve_rule_off 8913312 (zero-cost singleton rule on, lock
rule only, no dominated tests, no extensions): neos-3402294-bobin ends at 32,932 rows, 792
columns, 381,000 nonzeros, which is its size with all cheap rules on. The hypothesis of version
2 holds: R3 followed by the lock rule reaches bobin. M1 therefore has bobin as an acceptance
instance: at or above 0.95 of these three numbers, or traced before M2.
neos-3402454-bohle in the same run: 2,881,360 rows, 2,640 columns, 8,917,440 nonzeros (all
cheap rules: 2,881,228 / 2,508 / 8,915,592; the rest is the forcing column).

D. Fixes from the reviews of version 2 (v2_reviews.txt has the model and the fix for each)
Validity review, in the order of its list of open defects:
 1. (before the check part of M1) The postsolve test of the structure check, D(3): the check's
    own implementation of each undo is compared value by value with postsolve() at every test
    point; "the saved row holds" is required only where the rest of the point lies inside the
    range recomputed from the record; one extra point per interval record that needs a move.
    zs_undo is assigned to crush clause 3 and the oracle.
 2. (before M3) The oracle with a huge twin bound: the points of the twin solver are specified,
    a reduced point is postsolved only when check_point accepts it, skipped points are counted.
 3. (before M5) The objective test is one forward pass over the stack: lift the pair at a merge
    record, eliminate the variable at a defined record.
 4. (before the check part of M1) Clause 5 of the crush check: the allowance includes what the
    saved columns differ by between the postsolved and the base point, times |a_i| / |a|.
 5. (before M2) An alias of origin probing carries its row only when the row is deleted in that
    batch, else row = -1.
 6. (before the second step of M4) R8's force test for a continuous column uses the form of the
    forcing rule (rounding size), not the feasibility tolerance.
 7. (before the second step of M4) R8: one transaction per binary; the candidate that is the
    binary itself is tested afterwards on the new state.
 8. (M0) The wrong engine r_prop_inf is rewritten so that it also removes the new early return
    of candidate(); (M2) the six doubleton wrong engines follow the code that moves.
 9. (M1) R2 and R5: both ends of the implied range are written with the row side and the stored
    activity less the term of the column, as candidate() does; R4 undo: one rule, the minimum
    over the rows and the bound, written into the contract of the forcing record.
Worth-and-cost review:
 1. Scope: see A.
 2. The gate table gets the two lines "range_kept without bobin" and "lead counted as zero";
    the nine instances where the engine is ahead today are named in the M1 report first.
 3. Work: a count per row of its live singleton columns and of marked columns, so that a listed
    row without one costs one visit; ceilings are stated per family and in two parts (first
    call, later calls) and are reports only until M1 has measured them.
 4. Bobin fallback: settled by B.
 5. M0's time criterion is decided on the minimum of three alternating passes of the binary
    before and after on one machine, or in work units, not against a stored sum of seconds.
 6. Acceptance reference: see C.
 7. The price of the low-worth rules is stated net and gross; it decides their order only.

E. Decisions of version 2's list, taken as recommended until the owner says otherwise
 2. Merge of two integer columns: the three settings are built; the default is range_kept
    (an integer pair is merged only when the merged column gets the kept column's range back in
    the same step). Continuous pairs are always merged.
 3. The gate has the same two numbers in each of the three measures.
 4. R3 and R4 stay off in primal-only mode until the owner decides.
 5. R8 stays in the required set, as the second step of M4, with its own switch.
 6. Work ceilings and rounds: reports only until measured; max_rounds is not raised.

F. Changes that the design of the five further rules asks of R1 to R9 (lowworth_v2_design.txt,
   section CHANGES TO THE NINE-RULE DESIGN, which has the model for each; added 2026-11-27)
 1. (M0) Nothing to build. To confirm when M0 lands: the list masks are uint16_t (the five
    rules end at 13 row lists and 8 column lists), the option draw of gprs_fuzz and the other
    places that enumerate rules follow rule::count, an unknown record kind is an error.
 2. (M1) R2 and R5 for an integer column: the integrality predicate gets the drift guard of
    row tightening (src/rules_tightening.cpp:126-145) and lives in one internal function
    integral_for in src/integral.hpp, which the later rules call too. The drift is measured on
    the row divided by the coefficient of the column, as the cited code does for its scale.
    Model: x - 1.0000000005 y = 0, x an integer singleton column, y integer in [0, 2000000].
 3. (M1 and M5) Every undo, crush step, push assertion and check clause that depends on a
    column type reads it through postsolve_t::integer_at(col, position) (integer_now for a
    push). Until a conversion exists it returns the input type, so no result changes.
 4. (M1, structure check) next_anchor finds saved rows through the col_from_rows records, not
    by walking the saved-row pool: later records put rows into the same pool that must never
    be offered as anchors.
 5. (M3) R6's absorb test uses the shared implied test in its rounding-only form, not
    implied_tolerance: R6 keeps the absorber's bound and its row. R2 and R5 may keep
    implied_tolerance, their column leaves the model. Model: 10 p + 10 q - w = 0, p in
    [0, 0.5 - 1.5e-7], q in [0, 1] continuous with cost -1 each, w integer in [0, 5] with cost
    0.05 (the optimum goes from -0.25 to -0.2 with the tolerance band).
 6. (M4) R8 collects its rows after the column pass of the dual column family and reads "the
    row is no equation" and the lock counts at application.
 7. (M1 to M5) No family keeps a fact that depends on a column type across calls unless it
    follows the events of a type change. This includes the per-row counts of D (worth review
    item 3) if they count continuous singleton columns.
 8. Named so that it is not found late, not required: R3 could take an integer singleton
    column whose row is integral for it.
Also found there in existing Stage 1 code, to be repaired with M1's integral_for: the alias
forest's edge test (src/alias_forest.cpp:44-57) has no drift guard either.
