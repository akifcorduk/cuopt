[serious] R8 scans its lock row, up to 1000 entries, once for every column whose single lock is that row. Its cost entry calls this 'one scan of r of at most 1000 entries' per column.
  WHY: The work is the sum over rows of (single-lock columns of the row) times (row length), so up to 1000 times the nonzeros. It is spent on the budget that R2 to R5 and R7 share. Smallest model: 1000 binaries with negative cost, one knapsack row (<=) and one cover row (>=) over all of them. Every column has one up lock, in the knapsack row, and locks both ways. No binary releases the row, so every column scans 1000 entries and finds nothing: 10^6 units on 2,000 nonzeros. A pass is 2000 + 8 * 1002 = 10,016 units (E/include/gprs/presolve.hpp:69-76), so the stall limit of {8, 4, 1e5} is 140,064. The call stops after about 140 columns with no finding (E/include/gprs/rules.hpp:34). A round without a finding ends the rounds (E/src/presolve.cpp:280-284), so the columns behind are never tried by the O(1) rules R2 to R5 either, and the result is no fixed point. With 100 such blocks the call stops after about 4,100 of 100,000 columns. HiGHS has the same scan (H/HPresolveDualFixing.cpp:36, 62) but no budget.
  FIX: The release test (i) does not depend on x. Compute, once per call for each lock row and locking side, the list of (binary, value) pairs that release it; this is O(row length) and the list is usually empty. A column then pays O(1) plus the force test on that list, and the total is the nonzeros of the lock rows. Recompute the list for a row whose version changed. Also give R8 its own budget or run it after the singleton rules, so that a stall cannot starve them.

[serious] A chain of singleton columns costs one round per link. The column family visits its candidates 'each once per call, in index order', is called once per round, and max_rounds = 20 counts the rounds of the whole presolve (E/include/gprs/presolve.hpp:97, E/src/presolve.cpp:268).
  WHY: Smallest model: free continuous columns x_1..x_T; rows t = 1..T are equations x_(t-1) + x_t + (two other columns) = d_t, with no x_0 in row 1. Only x_T is a singleton. R2 removes x_T and row T, which makes x_(T-1) a singleton, but x_(T-1) was already visited in this call, so it waits for the next round. That gives T rounds. With T = 30 the presolve ends on max_rounds with ten rows and columns left and no fixed point. With the column order reversed the same model is finished in one call, so the result depends on the order. Each extra round also pays a full cascade, parallel rows, doubleton equations and their end_phase calls. HiGHS has no such limit: its singleton list is a work queue that grows while it is processed (H/HPresolve.cpp:4884, fed at :578). In the reference 2,200 of the 16,881 free column substitutions come after the first scan (stage.out): snp-02-004-104 1,224 of 1,556, highschool1-aigio 358 of 359, physiciansched3-3 187 of 194, supportcase42 140 of 140.   FIX: Inside one call of column_rules, append a column whose live size drops to 1 through a deletion made by this call to the call's candidates. Use a queue in event order, which is deterministic, and test the budget between two columns as now. The chain then costs its entries once and no extra round. Add as an M2 gate item the number of instances that end on max_rounds or lose the fixed-point flag through the new families.

[minor] The work bound of R1, and by the shared code of R8, is wrong: 'A column is substituted once, so the total over the presolve is at most the nonzeros.'
  WHY: A column is substituted once, but the binary inherits its entries and can itself be substituted later, which moves them again. Smallest model: binaries, rows k = 1..n with x_k + y_k - 2 x_(k+1) = 0, and x_1 in L further rows. In index order, row k substitutes x_k by x_(k+1) and moves the L inherited entries each time. That is n * L moves on about 3n + L nonzeros; with n = L = 10^4 it is 10^8 moves on 4 * 10^4 nonzeros. The budget {4, 2, 1e5} then stops the call every round and the chain is not finished in 20 rounds. In reverse row order each column moves once. The doubleton family avoids this by collecting a batch in the alias forest and substituting each column once into its root (E/src/rules_doubleton.cpp:191-243). The design takes that family's substitute() but not its batch. HiGHS substitutes at once as the design does, so this concerns our own bound, and the chain depth on real instances is not known.
  FIX: Collect the aliases of the listed rows of a call first, compose them (a column whose binary is itself aliased in the same call goes straight to the final binary), then substitute once per column. Otherwise state the bound as nonzeros times alias depth and report the depth at M1 on highschool1-aigio, brazil3 and neos-1445765.
