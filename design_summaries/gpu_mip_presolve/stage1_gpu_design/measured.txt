======== SUMMARY ========
I instrumented a scratch copy of the host engine (commit 04692babd) and measured width, depth, tail and whole-matrix shares on the 240 benchmark dumps and the 17 small dumps. The instrumented build gives the stored hash and every stored per-family count on all 240. Nothing was changed in the project tree. Seconds are from this Mac under load, not from the node.

Main results on the 240:
- **Whole-matrix parts:** state build, first propagation sweep, parallel-row keys, output and hash hold 57.2% of the engine seconds (60.3% on the 30 instances of at least 1e6 nonzeros). With the further whole-matrix scans it is 78.8% (82.1%).
- **Testing against applying:** calls without any finding hold 26.7% of the engine seconds. In the six families other than the doubleton rule, 91.8% of the work units lie outside the visits that change the model.
- **Depth:** dual fixing, parallel rows and the doubleton rule have chain length 1 in every call; row tightening at most 2, and that is the same row visited again. Row rules: 96.1% of the calls with a finding have a chain of at most 3 (longest 183). Propagation: 84.2% (longest 834, on neos-1122047). Calls with a chain above 3 hold 5.3% of the engine seconds. Driver rounds: median 2, largest 12.
- **Width:** 68.8% of the 7.2 million findings are at chain level 1. Under the reservation table as DESIGN.md section 4 writes it, only 34.2% fit the first batch; under the literal "shares no row and no column" rule, 29.7%. The doubleton rule fits only 32% of its trees into a first batch under any per-row rule, although no tree depends on another.
- **Tail:** calls below 20,000 work units are 64.9% of the calls but 0.6% of the engine seconds (5.6% below 5e4 nonzeros, 0.4% from 1e6). 72.8% of the calls take the host less than 85 microseconds; at one fixed device step each they would cost 4.4% of the engine seconds (63.7% below 5e4 nonzeros; 6.9 times the whole engine time on unitcal_7).

Not measured: the nine instances above 1e7 nonzeros, simple probing (commit edd147a7e), the column rules that are not built, and any device or Jacobi order.

=====================================================================
2. DEFINITIONS (what is counted)
=====================================================================
Call: one family.run() from the driver (P/gpu_presolve/src/presolve.cpp:240-260). List (generation): one take of the family's list inside a call (rules_rows.cpp:247-263, rules_propagation.cpp:201-221, rules_tightening.cpp:326-349; for the singleton-column rules one pass over both lists, rules_singleton_cols.cpp:556-589). Items: rows or columns examined that were alive at their turn.

Unit: one visit that changed the model. A unit can hold several findings as the engine counts them.
- Row rules (row_rules): one row visit (the row deleted, plus the bound changes of a singleton row or the fixings of a forcing row).
- Bound propagation (propagation): one row visit with at least one bound change.
- Row tightening (tightening): one row visit with at least one edit.
- Dual fixing by locks (dual_fixing): one fixed column.
- Doubleton equations (doubleton): one root with all the columns substituted into it (the engine applies them root by root, rules_doubleton.cpp:213-239).
- Parallel rows (parallel_rows): one listed row with every row it was merged with or subtracted from (rules_parallel_rows.cpp:774-887).
- Singleton-column rules (singleton_cols): one reduction (a free singleton column with its row; the zero-cost singleton columns of one row; a forcing column with its rows).

Cells that a unit reads or writes: a row (sides, entries, liveness); the entries of a column (entries, lock counts, length); the bounds and status of a column; the cost of a column; whether a column is a singleton column. Writes are registered by hooks in every mutating operation of state_ops.cpp (set_col_bound 199-219, fix_col 282-319, delete_row 358-391, set_row_lower/upper 393-417, shift_row_sides 419-437, scale_row 439-468, set_coefficient 470-490, delete_entry 492-520, move_entry 522-565, note_singleton 118-132). Reads are registered per family: a row visit of the row rules, propagation, tightening and the free singleton column reads its row and the bounds of all columns of the row (that is the activity); a visit that tightens exactly one column from a row does not count as reading that column's own bound; dual fixing reads entries, cost and bounds of its column; parallel rows read both rows and whether their columns are singleton columns; the doubleton unit reads entries, bounds and cost of the root and of each substituted column. Lists, events and recorded implied bounds are not cells.

Chain (depth): level of a unit = 1 + the largest level of an earlier unit of the same call that wrote a cell this unit read. This is read-after-write. It is an upper bound of "only possible because of an earlier finding" (I did not test the counterfactual), and the share at level 1 is a lower bound of what one sweep over the state at call entry would find.

"Shares" for the greedy batches, three rules, all computed:
- strict (the literal reading): two units share when both read or write anything of the same row or of the same column.
- reservation (the table of P/design_summaries/gpu_mip_presolve/DESIGN.md lines 532-536): a write of a cell conflicts with every read and write of that cell by another unit; reads do not conflict.
- merge: as reservation, but two writes of one cell conflict only when one is exclusive. Exclusive: every change of a row, a cost, the removal of a column, an entry appended to a column. Not exclusive: lock counts and lengths (integer counters), tombstones and values of single entries, bounds that tighten (a minimum or maximum). This classification is mine.
Greedy pass in host order: a unit takes the first batch that holds no unit it shares with. "Order kept": it also comes after the batch of every earlier unit that wrote a cell it read (so it is never placed before the change it was found on). "Order not kept" is the plain greedy pass of the task text. Batches are counted up to 64; beyond that the count is reported as "more than 64".

5.3 What these numbers mean, family by family.
- Dual fixing: every finding of a call is independent of every other under every rule.
- Parallel rows: no listed row's reduction reads anything another one wrote, in any of the 172 calls. The engine itself takes the key away from a row whose core changed in the call (rules_parallel_rows.cpp:802-826), so a call already works on the state at its entry. The conflicts under the reservation and the strict rule are deletions of rows that share a column, that is, two decrements of the same lock count and column length.
- Row tightening: a unit reads only its own row and the bounds of its columns, and no tightening unit writes a bound. So the level-2 units (2.7%) are the same row visited again after its own edit, by construction. Under the reservation rule only 23.2% of the units fit the first batch, because every edit write-claims the entries of its columns.
- Doubleton: no tree depends on another (chain 1), but two trees that substitute columns in the same row both rewrite that row. Under any rule that gives a row to one unit per batch, 32.0% of the trees fit the first batch and 13.2% of the calls need more than 64 batches (the 30: 28.8%, 47.4% of the calls). Per instance the first-batch share has quartiles 30.4%, 81.2%, 100%.
- Row rules: 62.7% of the units need nothing of the same call, 90.8% are within three levels. The reservation and strict rules put far fewer into a batch (30.5%, 27.5%) because deleted rows share columns. The largest number of units of one call on one column is 8,454 on map10 (row rules), 83,218 on neos-4532248-waihi (tightening), 47,521 on rd-rplusc-21 (parallel rows).
- Propagation: summed over the 240 only 37.9% of the units are at level 1 and 47.1% at level 8 or deeper, but three instances hold half of the units (neos-1122047 67,220; snp-02-004-104 63,656; neos-5114902-kasavu 22,568). Per instance (91 with a finding) the level-1 share has quartiles 21.3%, 69.5%, 100%. On the 30 it is 97.2%.
- Singleton columns: 94.2% at level 1; per instance the median is 100%.
- Per instance for the row rules (127 instances): quartiles of the level-1 share 69.0%, 100%, 100%; half of the units are on four instances (neos-3402294-bobin 544,236; map10 and map16715-04 278,698 each; physiciansched3-3 166,973).

11. WHAT I DID NOT CHECK, AND LIMITS
=====================================================================
- The chain is read-after-write, not a counterfactual: a unit that read a changed bound may have been possible without the change. The level-1 share is therefore a lower bound of what a sweep over the entry state finds, and the chain an upper bound of the true depth.
- No device order was simulated. The host applies changes at once inside a list; a sweep that reads only the entry state can need more or fewer sweeps than the chain says.
- A row's activity is modelled as a read of the bounds of all its columns. The bit pattern of an activity after several updates in another order is not modelled.
- The batch counts stop at 64.
- Seconds are from a loaded Mac, the smallest of 5 repetitions per record; sums of such minima are slightly below a real run (8.46 against 8.62 s).
- Simple probing on equations (commit edd147a7e) was not measured: the engine at 04692babd has no rule there, the family only takes its lists. The column rules that are designed and not built were not measured.
- Of the testbed below 1e7 nonzeros only the 17 local dumps were run.
- Why CMS750_4's doubleton call takes 0.038 s for 1,498 substitutions (274 ns per work unit) was not investigated.
