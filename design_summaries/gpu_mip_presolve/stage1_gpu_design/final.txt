'Methods that can be parallelized' is read as a property of the rule, not of today's order of application. A rule is parallel when its tests are a map over rows or columns on one state and its applications can be combined by a rule whose result does not depend on thread timing. Measured: 68.8% of 7.2 million findings need nothing of the same call, and dual fixing, parallel rows and the doubleton rule have chain length 1 in every call (240, measurement step); row rules and propagation run as rounds from one state with no instance larger (240, prototype). So the applications are parallel work and go to the device. Under the other reading (today's order is part of the method) the device can only test and the host applies; that is 'bulk-first': the same bits as today, about 1.9 times faster on the 240 and 1.7 times on the nine in the model. 'Smaller and non-parallel work to CPU' is a closed list: a model below a size threshold; steps below a work threshold where a host copy of the state exists; the driver; the postsolve undo; an alias forest of few edges and tree levels beyond a cap; a sequential test inside one row above a length cap. 2. ARCHITECTURE IN ONE PAGE

One engine, one state type. The library gprs and state_t stay. No second engine and no second state type is built.

A family call is a loop of steps. A step has four parts. FIND: a map over the listed rows or columns that reads the state as the step found it and writes candidates. SELECT: one of three rules decides which candidates are applied and how writes to one cell combine. APPLY: the writes. UPDATE: rows that lost or renamed an entry are rewritten in place, sizes, free sizes, singleton counts and locks of the touched lines are brought current by integer sums, rows whose activity can have changed are marked and folded again before their next reader, the list masks of the consumers are set, and one status record is produced (findings, records, work, the size and the entries of every next list, the outcome with the smallest index that caused it). Nothing is applied while another item of the same step is tested.

The three rules of SELECT.
- Combine (K1), for writes that hold for every feasible point and for integers. A bound is reduced over pairs (value, source index): the better value wins; among values that compare equal as doubles (so +0 and -0) the smaller source index wins; the bits of the winner are stored. That is the result of a loop in ascending source index with a strict comparison, and it is one result in bits on any tree. Integer rounding comes after the reduction. A lower bound above the upper by more than the tolerance is infeasible; a smaller crossing gives the column the upper candidate. A row that gave a bound is deleted in the step only when the bounds written to its columns are at least as tight as its own candidates (a forcing row: when every free column was written at its value); otherwise it stays, is listed again and is tested in the next step in its own units. Counters change by integer sums or integer atomics.
- Ordered fold (K2), for terms that several units add into one cell (the coefficient of a root, the side shift of a row, the cost of a column). The terms are brought together by a stable radix sort on the unique integer key (cell, source index), each run is added in ascending source index as a compensated sum, and tests such as the cancellation test are made once on the folded sum. The objective offset has no running sum: each column owns its contribution and the offset is formed once at the output by a tree fixed by the number of columns.
- Reservation (K3), for a unit that needs a cell alone. The unit writes its key into the cell with an integer atomic minimum; keys are unique over all units of the step. A unit is applied when it holds every cell it write-claims and no unit with a smaller key write-claims a cell it reads. One pass. A loser leaves no trace and stays on its list. The applied set is what a loop in ascending key gives when every premise is tested at application.
No floating-point atomic is used anywhere.

The state holds no history.
1. Activities are values: the fold of numerics.hpp (entry k of the row in storage order to lane k mod lanes, compensated lanes, the fixed merge tree) over the bounds of the moment, never updated. The counts of infinite terms are two uint16 arrays that saturate (only 0, 1 and 'more' are read); the position of a single infinite entry is not stored, the entry recognises its own term.
2. Work lists are sets, served in ascending index.
3. The layout is not state. A rebuild is decided on counters (entries deleted since the last rebuild over the entries at that rebuild), not on slots.
4. A live row has no dead entry and holds its entries in ascending column index. A step that deletes or renames an entry of a row rewrites that row in place in the same step. So the lane of an entry is its slot position, on both executors, after every step. This is possible because no rule of Stage 1 lets a row grow (the two places that add an entry reuse a slot of the same row). Columns are in ascending row index and may hold dead entries until they are rebuilt; only order-free reductions run over a column.
5. A new entry of a column (it arises in two rules: a root takes over an entry, and the singleton column of a subtracted equation enters the target row) is merged into its column at the end of the step that made it. Nothing waits for a rebuild.
6. Records are ordered by step, then by a fixed order of record kinds, then by unit index; pool offsets are prefix sums in that order.
7. Budgets are tested between steps; a step is done in full or not at all.

Two executors. The host executor is plain C++17 in the host library and is the whole engine in a build without CUDA. The device executor is CUDA code in G/device. The arithmetic of every rule lives in shared headers of value functions (numbers in, numbers out) that both compile, with contraction off and the one explicit fused multiply-add of two_prod. Both hold the same arrays with the same slots, so a hand-over is a copy and 'device equals host' is tested array by array.

The driver is host code. It runs today's sequence (start step; rounds of cascade, parallel rows, doubleton, simple probing and the column families; the switch to explicit bounds; absorption of fixed terms; output; hash) whichever executor works, and chooses the executor of each step from work counts (section 4). A cascade pass stays four family calls, each a loop of steps until its list is empty; the fused wave of 'device-native' is not taken now (section 12).

Today's order stays selectable on the host. With the rules split into evaluation and application, the option apply_order = sequential runs 'evaluate, then apply at once' per unit in ascending index, and apply_order = batch runs the step. The device runs the batch order only; identity of host and device is claimed for it.

Where the state lives. On the device from the build to the output when the device is used. The host holds the input model, the options, the status records, the record stack after each flush and the reduced model. A host copy of the state exists only in the hand-back regime for small states and under the transitional rule during the build-up.

3. THE PARTS (where each runs, the device algorithm, the conflict rule, the measured reason for what stays on the host; the full entries are in the field per_part)

3.1 State build. Both: device from device_min_nnz (provisional 100,000 input nonzeros). Upload from pageable memory; column histogram and scan; transpose with twins by the stable radix sort that exists (G/device/src/state_build.cu, bit-equal in 3,734 builds); locks; row singleton counts; both activities by fold kernels in the host's lane order with the counts of infinite terms taken in the scan; masks; scheduling lists by length class. No conflict. Host below the threshold: a whole presolve takes the host 1.7 ms in the median below 5e4 nonzeros (93 of the 240) and 15.3 ms from 5e4 to 2e5 (50 instances). 31.4% of the node seconds on the 240, 34.2% on the nine.
3.2 Start step. With the executor of the state build: two maps, fixed columns recorded by a compaction in ascending column. No conflict. 0.2% of the seconds (240).
3.3 Row rules (empty, infeasible, redundant, singleton and forcing row). Both. FIND: class per listed row in O(1) from the stored activities, a scan only for singleton rows and accepted forcing rows. Bounds of singleton rows and fixings of forcing rows by the combine rule with the crossing rule; deletions by flag with integer lock and size updates. Host for the tail: lists below 100 rows are 1,458 of 2,263 lists with 0.1% of the work (240).
3.4 Bound propagation and the switch to explicit bounds. Both. One Jacobi round per step: candidates per entry from the stored activity (residual first, error guard, the single infinite term), reduced per column by the combine rule in a column-driven or a row-driven form chosen by counts; the best candidate of the round becomes the bound or the implied record. The switch to explicit bounds is a map over columns. Host for the tail: lists below 1,000 rows are 1,445 of 2,122 lists with 8.7% of the propagation work (240).
3.5 Row tightening. Both. A map over rows; a row edits only itself; the scale is the least common multiple of per-entry denominators. No conflict. Host for the tail: 688 of 1,278 lists have fewer than 100 rows and 1.4% of the work (240).
3.6 Dual fixing by locks. Both. A map over columns; fixings together; records by compaction. No conflict (one batch in all 457 calls with a finding, 240).
3.7 Parallel rows with row addition. Both. Keys as an integer sum per row, stable radix sort, up to parallel_bucket_limit rounds of exact comparison with a bucket leader, a merge step (sides by the combine rule), then on the merged state a subtraction step (an equation with a singleton column serves one target per step by reservation; targets rewrite themselves). Host for later calls: the first call holds 91.5% of the work (240) and 95.6% (nine).
3.8 Doubleton rule and the alias pipeline (shared with simple probing and the single-lock alias). Both. Edges by a map; the forest is the minimum spanning forest under a unique key (union-find on the host, Boruvka rounds on the device); maps composed level by level from the root in today's association order; all trees substituted in one step by the ordered fold; touched rows rewritten in column order; new root entries merged into their columns. No row is reserved (under one tree per row only 32.0% of 280,666 trees fit a first batch, 240). Host: the forest of a small batch (tree walk and composition are 0.018 s of this family's 0.317 Mac seconds on the 240, about 37 ns per alias, against about a millisecond of launches) and levels beyond 64 (0.3% of the substituted columns; depth 741 on CMS750_4).
3.9 Singleton-column family. Both, in two steps per pass: row units (a row unit writes only its own row; costs of shared columns by the ordered fold), then forcing columns on the state after the row units (reservation on rows, key = column index). Host for later passes: the first pass of the first call holds 80.3% of the work and 97.5% of the findings (240).
3.10 Simple probing on equations (built at edd147a7e, review to be finished first). Both. Collection is a map on an unchanged state; all edges go to the alias pipeline; a row is deleted when all its edges became maps. Host for small lists: 277,320 aliases on 8 of the 240 in the one stored Mac run.
3.11 The nine designed rules (dominance between parallel columns and their merge, dual bound strengthening, single-lock alias, implied equation, implied integer with scaling, column stuffing, the single-equation extension of dual fixing, the dominated column). Both; each is written once as evaluation plus application, run on the host first in the design's index order and then as a step, on the kernels of the families it resembles. The conflict rule of each is a proposal in per_part and is fixed only after a table of the cells the rule reads and writes has been checked against its Stage 1c text and the step form has passed a validity review. Host parts: short lists (not measured, the rules do not exist) and the prefix test of column stuffing inside one row.
3.12 Driver. Host. One status record per device step through one read-back (13.8 microseconds on one H200). It is a few comparisons per step: 707 rounds, about 6,900 cascade steps and 1,367 calls of the three structural families on the 240 in all.
3.13 Storage. With the executor that owns the state: rows rewritten in the step; column insert merge at the end of a step (the whole column store by the radix sort when the batch is a large share of the nonzeros); rebuild on counters (row store compacted, fixed terms folded into the sides, column store by the radix sort at 467 microseconds + 0.279 ns per nonzero).
3.14 Record stack and postsolve. Records are produced by the executor of the step, in device arrays with prefix-sum offsets, flushed at a hand-over, at a byte limit and at the end. Undo and crush stay on the host: the undo is a chain by definition and runs outside the presolve call.
3.15 Output, with the removal of empty rows and the comparison with the input for the status 'unchanged'. Both, as a step under the hybrid rule: two scans, a gather with the new column index (no sort), sides less the folded fixed terms, the offset by its tree; delivered as device arrays to the cuOpt adapter or downloaded.
3.16 Hash. Both, or off: per array an integer sum of mixed (position, word), the array sums chained on the host. The chain hash of today is serial by definition and stays as an option.

4. THE HYBRID RULE

The driver chooses the executor of every step from numbers that are functions of the model and the settings. Nothing is read from the machine. Because both executors give the same bits, no value below can change a reduced model, a record or a work count; that claim is itself tested by forcing each value low and high and by drawing them at random. Work of a step: W = sum over the lines of its list of (8 + live entries), today's unit, known before the step from the previous status record.

H0. Build without CUDA, or executor = host: everything on the host.
H1. Small models. Input nonzeros below device_min_nnz (provisional 100,000): the host does the whole call and nothing is uploaded. Measured reason: the median host time of a whole presolve is 1.7 ms below 5e4 nonzeros (93 of the 240) and 15.3 ms from 5e4 to 2e5 (50); 72.8% of all family calls take the host less than one fixed device step of 85 microseconds, and at one step each they would cost 63.7% of the engine seconds below 5e4 nonzeros (240, Mac seconds against an H200 figure). The requirement 'better than HiGHS from 50,000 nonzeros' is carried by the host path there. The value is fixed from a stored crossover measurement (device forced against host, per part and size class) and may not exceed 200,000 without the owner's consent.
H2. From device_min_nnz the device builds the state and owns it until the output. Every step runs on the device unless H3 or H5 says otherwise.
H3. Hand-back, only while the state is at most mirror_max_bytes (provisional 16 MiB: 42 of the 126 instances of the 240 from 1e5 nonzeros). When the next step is below T_down of its family in two consecutive steps, the state goes to the host executor; it returns for a step of at least T_up. Output plus hash is a step with W = live nonzeros. Provisional values, the ones the critics simulated: T_down 20,000 units for the cascade families and 200,000 for the structural ones, T_up 200,000 for every family. Measured reason for a hand-back: calls below 20,000 units are 64.9% of the calls and 0.6% of the engine seconds (240), and unitcal_7 (127,595 nonzeros, 1,398 family calls in 33 ms) would take 6.9 times its engine time at one device step per call.
H4. Above mirror_max_bytes there is no hand-back; small steps run on the device at their fixed cost. Reason: on the 30 the calls below one fixed step cost 0.3% of the engine seconds at one step each (measured host seconds, H200 step cost); and a host copy is not free: created inside the call it costs 1.3 to 1.4 s on the 106 instances of the 240 that hand over under the rule of 'one algorithm' (first touch 2.7 GB/s, pinning 0.38 s per GB), and with it counted the class from 2e5 to 1e6 nonzeros is 2.0 times faster than today with a hand-over and 4.0 times with every step on the device (model; 2.3 times with fixed costs doubled). Only between 1e5 and 2e5 nonzeros the hand-over wins in the model (1.6 against 1.0 times).
H5. Small sequential objects go to the host at any size, never the state: an alias forest below forest_device_min_edges (provisional 100,000; the edge list goes down, the maps come up), tree levels beyond forest_level_cap (provisional 64), and a sequential test inside one row above its length cap.
H6. Form of a candidate step: column-driven or row-driven, by an integer comparison of the entries of the touched columns with the entries of the listed rows (the status record carries both). Reason: the touched columns hold as many entries as the whole matrix from a median of 4,853, 13,211 and 40,528 frontier entries in the classes 5e4 to 2e5, 2e5 to 1e6 and from 1e6 (147 input matrices, performance critic).
H7. Memory: when plan_memory(rows, columns, nonzeros, options) exceeds the byte budget, the whole call runs on the host. After a failed device allocation the call runs on the host with a logged warning (a switch makes it an error).
H8. Transitional: a step that has no device form yet runs on the host through a whole-state hand-over at any size, with its seconds reported. At acceptance no step uses H8.

All values are options with one shipped default each, fixed once at M12 by the rule of AS-307 on the 240 and the 40 (smallest summed time, ties to the host, no instance slower than the host path by more than the larger of 5% and 2 ms), with three settings of H3 measured: no hand-back, hand-back with the host copy created in the call, hand-back with a warm pool. The per-family break-even differs by a factor of nine (host cost per work unit on the 240, node: propagation 9.7 ns, doubleton 7.2, row rules and tightening 5.1, parallel rows 3.4, singleton columns 2.1, dual fixing 1.1), so T_down is set per family. Fixed costs behind the rule (Stage 0, one H200): launch with synchronisation 9.0 microseconds, with an 8-byte read-back 13.8, reduce, scan or compaction up to 1e5 elements 23 to 46, sort of 1e3 to 1e5 elements 94 to 501, ordered hand-back at most about 141, pageable copy about 10 GB/s. The designers put a cascade round at 100 to 200 microseconds and the performance critic at 190 to 250; structural steps at 1 to 3.5 ms. None is measured; M0 measures mock steps.
What the rule gives on recorded host steps (completeness critic's simulation, 114 instances of the 240 from 1e5 nonzeros with a state up to 128 MiB, hand-back after two low steps and return at 200,000): 98.7% of the rule work on the device, 2.2 switches per instance; the rule of 'one algorithm' as written gave 84.4%.

5. THE IDENTITY RULE, AND WHAT IS LOST OR GAINED IN REDUCTIONS

With and without a GPU: equal in every bit. For the same model and settings the reduced model, the record stack, every statistic counted in work units or findings, the status and the hash of the state after every step are the same for a build without CUDA, the host executor forced, the device executor forced, the hybrid rule with any thresholds, either candidate form, the pinned path on x86 and the managed path on Grace Hopper, any GPU type, any run and any co-running load. Reasons: one function of values per rule; one fold order for every floating-point sum; reductions under a total order; sorts on unique integer keys; integer atomics only; no library reduction of a floating-point sum (CCCL guarantees the same sum across GPU types only for the plain reduction, not the segmented one; read by the research step). What differs by design: seconds and the device counters. I agree with the preference for the same bits: it lets the oracle, the structure and crush checks, the wrong engines and the campaign test the device path through the host path, and it makes every threshold a matter of time only.

Against today's engine (commit 04692babd, after the triage of the 18 oracle failures): with every switch at its old position the engine is today's, bit for bit, for as long as the old forms are kept. With the new positions it is not equal in bits; the gate is on reductions and on strength. Measured so far, all on the 240 benchmark dumps on the host: activities as values 0 of 238 hashes differ (square41 and square47 not finished); least-common-multiple scale 0 of 8,842,639 row searches and 0 of 240 hashes differ; ascending lists 2 of 240 hashes differ; batch rounds for row rules and propagation with ascending lists and budgets between rounds 6 of 240 hashes differ, no instance larger in rows, columns or nonzeros, momentum1 65 rows smaller and neos-1122047 2 rows smaller, rounds +5.7% (row rules) and +0.9% (propagation), work +2.8%; the crossing rule on top of it 0 of 240 differ. Not measured: parallel rows by leaders, the alias batch with ordered sums and sorted rows, the singleton-column family in two steps, simple probing through the forest, the nine and 14 of the 31. The field reductions_lost lists where a loss is possible.

Options and statistics that keep their name and change their meaning: the budget stop (between two lines today, between two steps then; work, first_work, calls, max_cascade_passes and max_rounds keep their meaning); parallel_bucket_limit (unlike neighbours per row today, leader rounds per bucket then); rebuild_dead_fraction (share of dead slots today, deleted entries since the last rebuild then); phase_hashes (extended to steps); the result hash (hash_kind). New statistics: steps per family, steps on the device, hand-overs, bytes moved, launches, read-backs, seconds of upload, download, hand-over and landing.

6. THE DEVICE STATE

Matrix. The line stores of state_t on both sides: per line start (int64), used, capacity, size (int32); per slot index (int32, -1 for a dead entry), value (double), twin (int64). Storage is in index order; two scheduling lists (rows and columns by length class: six classes up to 64 entries as lane groups of a warp, one class up to 8,192 as one block of 256 threads, longer lines with the same block for the fold and chunks for order-free reductions), made by a stable partition after a build, a rebuild or a column merge, give the kernels their classes. Physically binned storage is a benchmarked variant that cannot change a result. Rows: sides, alive, free size, singleton count, version, mask, and per side of the activity hi, lo (its own array), largest term, uint16 count of infinite terms. Columns: bounds, cost, type, status, locks, implied-bound records, mask, family data, the column's contribution to the offset. About 40 bytes per nonzero, 100 per row and 90 per column persistent; sort scratch up to 32 bytes per nonzero for a full rebuild (2.57e8 nonzeros: 10.3 GB plus 8.2 GB). Sizes are planned from the model before the first allocation.
Deletions. A deleted row is a flag; its entries in the columns are dead without a write and are skipped by a look-up of the row flag. A row that loses or renames an entry is rewritten in place by its own thread group in the same step, with the twins of the moved entries corrected. Lock and size changes are integer atomics over the entries of what is deleted, so a deletion costs what it deletes.
Work lists are the masks; a step compacts its mask into an index buffer or tests the flag in a kernel over all lines (the same result). Record stack: device arrays per kind, the order array and the pool of saved entries, flushed to the host at a hand-over, at a byte limit and at the end (dlr2 saves 71 million entries, 0.85 GB).
Memory paths. x86: device memory; in the hand-back regime the host state takes its arrays from a pool of the presolve's memory resource, and a hand-over is a whole copy first and ordered dirty records later only if the measurement at M7 shows that the regime pays. Grace Hopper: the same arrays in one managed slab with preferred location GPU and accessed-by host; host_access waits for the stream and returns the array itself; cudaMalloc memory is used for nothing the host must read. That path is functional only until a node is measured and is tested on x86 by forcing managed memory. The host arrays of every path come from the presolve's memory resource, so that device-built arrays are moved in without a zero fill and huge pages can be asked for (with huge pages the host constructor goes from 34 to 24 s on 15 instances, HISTORY Entry 32). Without CUDA the host state on ordinary memory and the host executor are the whole engine.

--- 3
part: Row rules (empty row, infeasible row, redundant row, singleton row, forcing row)
runs_on: Both. Device when no host copy exists or the step is above its threshold (provisional 20,000 work units), host for the tail in the hand-back regime.
device_algorithm: FIND: a kernel over the listed rows gives each a class in O(1) from the stored activities, in today's order of tests; a singleton row and an accepted forcing row are scanned by the lane group or block that owns the row; a singleton row stores its two bound candidates, a forcing row the value of each free column. SELECT and APPLY: the candidates are reduced per column by the combine rule on pairs (value, source row), column-driven by the thread group of the column or row-driven by a sort of the candidates by column and a reduction by key (the form is chosen by counts); a row that gave a bound is deleted only when the bounds written are at least as tight as its candidates, a forcing row only when every free column was written at its value, otherwise the row is listed again; deleted rows are a flag, their entries change lock and size counts by integer atomics, new singleton columns are flagged; fixed columns are recorded by a compaction. One step is one round; the call repeats while its list is not empty.
why_host: The tail. Lists below 100 rows are 1,458 of 2,263 lists with 0.1% of the work; 96.1% of the calls with a finding have a chain of at most 3 and the longest is 183 (irish-electricity, 172 rounds in batch form) (240, measurement step and prototype).
same_result_as_today: Not bit for bit: the same tests on the same arithmetic, but on the state at the start of the round. Measured together with propagation in the same form (prototype, 240 benchmark dumps): hash equal on 234, no instance larger, rounds +5.7%, work +1.1%; with the crossing rule added the hashes equal that run on 240 of 240.
conflict_rule: Combine rule (K1) with the crossing rule. A crossing beyond the tolerance is infeasible; at a smaller crossing the column takes the upper candidate and the row whose candidate was cut stays and is tested in the next step in its own units. Two forcing rows that want one column at different values follow the same rule.
expected_gain: 5.1% of the node seconds on the 240; the first list of the first call holds 72.7% of its work and lists of at least 10,000 rows 92.8%.

--- 4
part: Bound propagation and the switch to explicit bounds
runs_on: Both. Device when no host copy exists or the step is above its threshold (provisional 20,000 work units), host for the tail in the hand-back regime.
device_algorithm: One Jacobi round per step. Activities are stored values, so no fold runs inside the round. Per entry of a listed row and readable side the candidate of candidate_of_finite_rest (implied.hpp): residual first, error guard, and the entry whose own term is the single infinite term takes the finite sum as it is. Per column and side the best candidate with the smallest source row among equals, column-driven over the scheduling lists (structure of cuOpt's load-balanced kernels: classes by length, shared warps for short columns, a split of very long columns with a second combining kernel, which is allowed because maximum and minimum do not depend on order) or row-driven (candidates of the listed entries, stable sort by column, reduction by key). APPLY: an integer column is rounded and tightened; a continuous column takes the best candidate of the round as its implied record when it beats the valid record by the step, or after the switch as its explicit bound, at most 16 times per bound; a crossing is infeasible by the smallest index. The switch to explicit bounds is a map over the columns with a valid record, plus the marking of their rows. The review rules of cuOpt PR 2372 are kept.
why_host: The tail. Lists below 1,000 rows are 1,445 of 2,122 lists and hold 8.7% of the propagation work on the 240 (4.5% on the 30 instances from 1e6 nonzeros); the longest call has 209 lists of a median of 2,608 rows (neos-1122047). After single bound changes a marked sequential code is often faster (Sofranac, Gleixner, Pokutta 2022, section 5, read by the research step, not by me).
same_result_as_today: Not bit for bit. Measured with the row rules (prototype, 240 dumps): hash equal on 234, two instances smaller, none larger, rounds +0.9%, work +16.7%, of which square41, square47 and neos-1122047 are 98%. Which candidate becomes a record is now defined (the best of the round) instead of the first in visit order.
conflict_rule: Combine rule (K1): maximum and minimum per column under the total order (value, then source row); marks are stores of a constant.
expected_gain: 16.4% of the node seconds on the 240, 23.1% on the 31, 10.1% on the nine; the highest host cost per work unit (9.7 ns). Published for the Jacobi form: 7.4 times over a sequential code on a V100 on 786 instances without transfers (Sofranac et al. 2022, read by the research step). Not measured here.

runs_on: Both. Device when no host copy exists or the step is above its threshold (provisional 20,000 work units), host for the tail in the hand-back regime.
device_algorithm: A map over the listed rows by lane groups and blocks: per entry its own denominator (the continued fraction of today, limit 1000), the least common multiple and the gcd as integer reductions, the drift as a lane fold, the sides rounded, coefficient strengthening per entry from the stored activity and the new side as a lane fold of the changed terms. The row writes its own values and, through the twins, the values in the columns (distinct slots), and its sides; a row that loses an entry is rewritten in place by its own group. An edited row is listed again; its second visit is the next step.

--- 7
part: Parallel rows: merge of parallel rows, subtraction of an equation from a row parallel to it up to singleton columns
runs_on: Both. Device when no host copy exists or the call is above its threshold (provisional 200,000 work units), host for later calls in the hand-back regime.
device_algorithm: Keys: a map over the listed rows with two reductions, the entry of the smallest core column and the integer sum of the mixes of (column, coefficient scaled by that entry, 20 mantissa bits). Stable radix sort of (hash, core length, row); bucket bounds by a comparison of neighbours and a scan. Up to parallel_bucket_limit leader rounds: the leader of a bucket is its smallest row without a class; every other such row compares its core exactly with the leader's (rows are in column order, so core entry k is compared with core entry k: a map with an and-reduction). Merge step: in a class without singleton columns the smallest row is kept, its sides are the combine rule over the images of all sides, the other rows are deleted, records in ascending removed row. Then, on the merged state, the subtraction step: pairs by today's rule (of two equations the one with fewer singleton columns, then the smaller index); an equation with a singleton column serves one target per step, the smallest; an equation without one is only read and serves all its targets; the target row is rewritten in place by its own group (core entries out, the one new entry in column order), the side shift by the ordered fold; the new entry is merged into its column at the end of the step.
why_host: Later calls: the first call holds 91.5% of the work on the 240 and 95.6% on the nine; 63.5% of the calls find nothing.
same_result_as_today: Not bit for bit. Classes of mutually parallel rows are merged into their smallest row with the same sides. Buckets with more unlike classes than the limit, or rows that are parallel only within the tolerance, can pair differently, in either direction. Not measured. No reduction of today reads what another wrote in the same call (chain 1 in all 172 calls with a finding), and there are 433,141 exact comparisons for 395,950 rows that have company in their bucket (240), so unlike rows in one bucket are rare.
conflict_rule: Merge: combine rule (K1) on the sides of the kept row. Subtraction: reservation (K3) on an equation that has a singleton column; a target writes only itself. Pivots are chosen among the rows the merge kept.
expected_gain: 10.2% of the node seconds on the 240 and 13.9% on the nine (12.25 s). Of 0.921 Mac seconds on the 240 the keys are 0.576 s, the sort and the links 0.161 s, comparisons and reductions 0.148 s.

--- 8
part: Doubleton equations with the alias forest and the substitution (the alias pipeline that simple probing and the single-lock alias share)
runs_on: Both. Collection and substitution on the device when no host copy exists or the batch is above its threshold (provisional 200,000 work units). The forest is built on the host below forest_device_min_edges (provisional 100,000) with only the edge list moved; tree levels beyond 64 are finished on the host.
device_algorithm: FIND: a map over the listed equations with two free entries gives edges with the unique key (row, substituted column). Forest: the minimum spanning forest under that key, which is unique: union-find over the edges in ascending key on the host, Boruvka rounds with an integer minimum per component and pointer jumping on component numbers only on the device. Root of a tree by today's key (integer before continuous, most admissible edges, most live entries, smallest index) as a reduction of an integer tuple; maps composed level by level from the root, parents before children, which is today's association order; an inadmissible edge makes its child the root of its own subtree. Bound transfer to the roots by the combine rule; the children of a root that the transfer fixes are fixed at their image and get no alias record. Substitution of all trees in one step: the entries of the substituted columns are emitted as (row, root, origin column, moved value, side product), sorted by (row, root, origin), the runs folded with the existing root entry first and one cancellation test; side shifts per row and costs per root by the ordered fold; the touched rows rewritten in place in column order; map rows deleted; columns removed; alias records in ascending column; root columns that gained entries merged at the end of the step (the whole column store by the radix sort when the batch is a large share of the nonzeros).
why_host: Small batches and deep trees. 65.6% of the calls find nothing; tree walk and composition are 0.018 s of 0.317 Mac seconds on the 240, about 37 ns per alias, against about a millisecond of launches for a device forest. 72.1% of 485,837 substituted columns are at depth 1 and 0.3% beyond 64; the deepest tree is a path of depth 741 on CMS750_4 (240).
same_result_as_today: Not bit for bit. The tree edges are today's when today's list is ascending (argued from alias_forest.cpp:84-105, not tested). A coefficient, a side or a cost that takes several terms is one fold in ascending origin with one cancellation test, where today it is a running plain sum with a test on every partial sum; rows stay in column order, where today the root takes the slot of the first substituted entry. Not measured.
conflict_rule: Ordered fold (K2); no row is reserved. Under any rule that gives a row to one tree per batch only 32.0% of 280,666 trees fit a first batch and 13.2% of 151 calls need more than 64 batches (240), although no tree depends on another.
expected_gain: 4.0% of the node seconds on the 240 and 5.9% on the nine (5.2 s, 7,077,830 aliases, neos-4332810-sesia alone 4.35 s); 79% of its host time is row deletion and substitution.

--- 9
part: Singleton-column family (free singleton column on an equation and on an inequality, zero-cost singleton columns, forcing column)
runs_on: Both. Device when no host copy exists or the step is above its threshold (provisional 200,000 work units), host for later passes in the hand-back regime.
device_algorithm: Two steps per pass (designed in this step). Step A, row units: per listed row with a singleton column one scan by the group that owns the row (its singleton columns in index order, the fixed terms, the two largest coefficients, the implied test from the stored activity); the unit of a row as today: the free singleton column with the smallest index that passes, else the zero-cost set. A row unit writes only its own row. The cost changes of the other columns of pivot rows are folded per column in ascending pivot row; growth factors by a maximum; saved rows are copied into the pool at offsets from an exclusive scan in unit order; of the units in ascending row the longest prefix that fits the saved-entry limit is accepted; rows that lose entries are rewritten in place. Step B, forcing columns, on the state after step A is applied and folded: an O(1) test per listed column; a forcing column claims every row it deletes with its column index. Records in unit order.
why_host: Later passes are narrow: the first pass of the first call holds 80.3% of the work and 97.5% of the findings; 81.8% of the calls find nothing; the longest chain is 30 (240).
same_result_as_today: Not bit for bit. The same tests per row. A column that becomes a singleton column through a deletion of the same call is served one step later (92.8% of the calls with a finding have a chain of at most 3). Costs with several terms are folded in row order. ======== HYBRID_RULE ========
The driver chooses the executor of every step from numbers that are functions of the model and the settings: input sizes and the work counts the engine keeps anyway. Nothing is read from the machine (no free memory, GPU type, core count or clock). Because both executors give the same bits, none of the values below can change a reduced model, a record or a work count; they change time only, and that is tested by forcing each low and high and by drawing them at random. Work of a step: W = sum over the lines of its list of (8 + live entries), today's unit, known before the step from the previous status record.

H0. Build without CUDA, or executor = host: everything on the host.
H1. Small models: input nonzeros below device_min_nnz (provisional 100,000): the host does the whole call, nothing is uploaded. Measured reason: a whole presolve takes the host 1.7 ms in the median below 5e4 nonzeros (93 of the 240 benchmark instances, node) and 15.3 ms from 5e4 to 2e5 (50 instances); 72.8% of all family calls take the host less than one fixed device step of 85 microseconds, and at one step each they would cost 63.7% of the engine seconds below 5e4 nonzeros (240, Mac seconds against an H200 figure). 'Better than HiGHS from 50,000 nonzeros' is carried by the host path there. The value is fixed from a stored crossover measurement (device forced against host, per part and size class) and may not exceed 200,000 without the owner's consent.
H2. From device_min_nnz the device builds the state and owns it until the output; every step runs on the device unless H3 or H5 says otherwise.
H3. Hand-back, only while the state is at most mirror_max_bytes (state bytes = 40 per nonzero + 100 per row + 90 per column; provisional 16 MiB, which covers 42 of the 126 instances of the 240 from 1e5 nonzeros, the largest with 384,329 nonzeros). When the next step is below T_down of its family in two consecutive steps, the state is handed to the host executor; it returns for a step of at least T_up. Output plus hash is a step with W = live nonzeros. Provisional values, the ones the critics simulated: T_down 20,000 units for row rules, propagation, tightening and dual fixing, 200,000 for parallel rows, the alias pipeline and the singleton columns; T_up 200,000 for every family. Measured reason: calls below 20,000 units are 64.9% of the calls and 0.6% of the engine seconds (240); unitcal_7 (127,595 nonzeros, 1,398 family calls in 33 ms) would take 6.9 times its engine time at one device step per call.
H4. Above mirror_max_bytes there is no hand-back: small steps run on the device at their fixed cost. Reasons: on the 30 instances of the 240 from 1e6 nonzeros the calls below one fixed step cost 0.3% of the engine seconds at one step each; a host copy created inside the call costs 1.3 to 1.4 s on the 106 instances of the 240 that hand over under the rule of the design 'one algorithm' (first touch 2.7 GB/s, pinning 0.38 s per GB), 42 to 44% on top of the modelled time; with that counted the class from 2e5 to 1e6 nonzeros is 2.0 times faster than today with a hand-over and 4.0 times with every step on the device (model; 2.3 times with fixed costs doubled), and the class from 1e6 to 1e7 is 4.8 against 10.8 times. Only between 1e5 and 2e5 nonzeros the hand-over wins in the model (1.6 against 1.0 times).
H5. Small sequential objects go to the host at any size, never the state: an alias forest below forest_device_min_edges (provisional 100,000; the edge list goes down, the maps come up; tree walk and composition cost the host about 37 ns per alias on the 240), tree levels beyond forest_level_cap (provisional 64; 0.3% of the substituted columns on the 240 are deeper), and a sequential test inside one row above its length cap.
H6. Form of a candidate step: column-driven or row-driven (candidates of the listed entries, stable sort by column, reduction by key), chosen by an integer comparison of the entries of the touched columns with the entries of the listed rows; the factor is measured in M7. Reason: the touched columns hold as many entries as the whole matrix from a median of 4,853, 13,211 and 40,528 frontier entries in the classes 5e4 to 2e5, 2e5 to 1e6 and from 1e6 (147 input matrices of the 240, performance critic), so a threshold in work units of the list misjudges a column-driven step on a large matrix.
H7. Memory: when plan_memory(rows, columns, nonzeros, options) exceeds the byte budget the whole call runs on the host. After a failed device allocation the call runs on the host with a logged warning; a switch makes it an error.
H8. Transitional: a step without a device form runs on the host through a whole-state hand-over at any size, with its seconds reported; at acceptance no step uses it.

How the values are fixed: once, at M12, by the rule of AS-307 on the 240 and the 40 (smallest summed time, ties to the host, no instance slower than the host path by more than the larger of 5% and 2 ms on the smallest of three alternating runs), with three settings of H3 measured: no hand-back, hand-back with the host copy created in the call, hand-back with a warm pool. T_down is set per family, because the host cost per work unit differs by a factor of nine (240, node: propagation 9.7 ns, doubleton 7.2, row rules and tightening 5.1, parallel rows 3.4, singleton columns 2.1, dual fixing 1.1). One shipped set of defaults on every machine; lifting the cap of H3 on Grace Hopper, where a hand-back copies nothing, is a setting.

Fixed costs behind the rule (Stage 0, one H200): launch with synchronisation 9.0 microseconds; with an 8-byte read-back 13.8; reduce, scan or compaction up to 1e5 elements 23 to 46; sort of 1e3 to 1e5 elements 94 to 501; ordered hand-back with a host copy at most about 141; pageable copy about 10 GB/s. Estimates, not measurements: a cascade round 100 to 200 microseconds by the designers and 190 to 250 by the performance critic; a parallel-rows call, an alias batch or a rebuild 1 to 3.5 ms. M0 measures mock steps before any threshold is used.

What the rule gives on recorded host steps (completeness critic's simulation, 114 instances of the 240 from 1e5 nonzeros with a state up to 128 MiB, hand-back after two low steps, return at 200,000): 98.7% of the rule work on the device with 2.2 switches per instance; lowest instances ns1830653 67.8%, momentum1 76.8%, snp-02-004-104 84.9%. The rule of 'one algorithm' as written (return at sixteen times the hand-over threshold) gave 84.4%, with 21 instances below 50%.

Not known: the cost of a real step; whether a hand-back regime pays at all once its host copy is counted; how instances with long narrow chains above the cap behave (snp-02-004-104 and irish-electricity have 105 and 172 rounds in one call at 4.6e5 and 5.2e5 nonzeros); every figure for Grace Hopper and for a busy GPU.

======== IDENTITY_RULE ========
With and without a GPU: equal in every bit. For the same model and settings the reduced model, the record stack, every statistic counted in work units or findings, the status, and the hash of the state after every step are the same for: a build without CUDA; the host executor forced; the device executor forced; the hybrid rule with any thresholds; either form of a candidate step; the pinned path on x86 and the managed path on Grace Hopper; any GPU type; any run and any co-running load. It follows from the construction: one function of values per rule, compiled for both sides with contraction off and one explicit fused multiply-add; one fold order for every floating-point sum (the lane fold of numerics.hpp, the ordered fold for sums into one cell, a fixed tree for the objective offset); reductions of bounds under a total order on (value, source index), which also fixes the sign of a zero; sorts on unique integer keys; reservation by an integer minimum of unique keys; integer atomics only; no library reduction of a floating-point sum. It is tested, not assumed: step equality array by array, and equal step-hash sequences on the 240 benchmark instances and the 40 testbed instances on two GPU types and both memory paths. What differs between such runs by design: seconds and the device counters (launches, read-backs, bytes moved, hand-overs). I agree with the preference for the same bits with and without a GPU: it lets the oracle, the structure and crush checks, the wrong engines and the campaign test the device path through the host path, it locates a wrong kernel at one step, and it makes every threshold a matter of time only. Its price is that the host engine changes, because today's order of application cannot be reproduced by parallel work.

Scope of the claim: it holds for the batch order (apply_order = batch), which is the only order the device runs. With apply_order = sequential for a family, that family runs on the host on every machine, so the result is still a function of the model and the settings alone.

Against today's engine (commit 04692babd, after the triage of the 18 oracle failures of the last campaign): with every switch at its old position the engine is today's, bit for bit, and each milestone shows that with the stored hashes. With the new positions it is not equal in bits. Measured so far, all on the 240 benchmark dumps on the host: activities as values, 0 of 238 hashes differ (square41 and square47 not finished; completeness critic's probe); scale of row tightening as least common multiple, 0 of 8,842,639 row searches and 0 of 240 hashes differ (same probe); lists in ascending index, 2 of 240 differ (prototype); batch rounds for row rules and propagation with ascending lists and budgets between rounds, 6 of 240 differ, status equal on all, no instance larger, momentum1 65 rows and neos-1122047 2 rows smaller (prototype); the crossing rule on top of it, 0 of 240 differ from the batch run (validity critic's repaired prototype; I compared the result files). Not measured: parallel rows by leaders, the alias batch with ordered sums and rows in column order, the singleton-column family in two steps, simple probing through the forest, sums in a fixed order; nothing on the nine instances above 1e7 nonzeros and on 14 of the 31.

What replaces 'equals today's stored hash' once a switch is on: for the host executor, per switch, the reductions gate (geometric mean of rows, columns and nonzeros of new over today at most 1.001 on the 240 and the 40, every instance above 1.005 explained), a strength report for every instance whose hash differs (bounds and coefficients of the two reduced models), the counts of stopped and limited calls and of fixed points, the gate against the HiGHS reference, the oracle campaign, the structure and crush checks, the second presolve and the wrong engines; then new stored hashes. For the device executor nothing is replaced: it must equal the host executor after every step.

======== ACCEPTANCE ========
The GPU acceleration of Stage 1 is complete when all of the following hold, each measured on the named set and stored with the run identity. Figures called 'proposal' are for the owner to set after M7 has measured real steps; they come from measured shares and a model, not from a device run.

1. Classification. Every step of every part of section 3 has a device form. A step runs on the host only for a reason of this closed list, each with a figure measured on the finished engine: the model is below device_min_nnz; the state has a host copy and the step is below its threshold; the alias forest is below its edge threshold or a level is beyond the cap; a sequential test inside one row is above its length cap; the driver; the postsolve undo and crush (outside the presolve call); the chain hash when it is chosen by option. No step uses the transitional rule, that is, no step is on the host because its device form is missing.

2. The size threshold is bounded. device_min_nnz comes from a stored crossover measurement (device forced against host, per part and size class) and is at most 200,000 nonzeros unless the owner agrees to a larger value, so that 'complete' cannot be reached by raising it (117 of the 147 benchmark instances from 5e4 nonzeros lie below 1e6).

3. Identity. For the 240 benchmark instances and the 40 testbed instances (the nine above 1e7 nonzeros included), three runs each: the hash sequence after every step is the same for the build without CUDA, the host executor forced, the device executor forced, the default hybrid and thresholds drawn at random; on two GPU types; on the pinned and the managed path; on a Grace Hopper node. Zero differences.

4. Share of the work on the device, in the engine's work units, for the rules and for state build, storage, output and hash: proposal at least 95% in every size class from 2e5 nonzeros and at least 90% from device_min_nnz to 2e5; every instance below 80% is listed with its reason. (The simulation on recorded host steps gives 98.7% over 114 instances of the 240; M0 confirms or corrects it with step-level counts.)

5. Host seconds. The profile of a hybrid run by part (state arrays, storage, start step, each family, records, output, hash, hand-overs) is stored. Proposal: the host seconds outside the driver are at most 10% of the hybrid time in every size class from 2e5 nonzeros when the reduced model is delivered as device arrays; the landing of the reduced model, the records and the saved rows in host memory is reported next to it.

6. Nothing slower. Hybrid against the host path of the same engine, smallest of three alternating runs, warm call (context and pools exist; the cold start is reported apart): no instance of the 240 and the 40 is slower by more than the larger of 5% and 2 ms.

7. Time against today's engine (commit 04692babd after the triage fix, same node). Proposals: the nine instances above 1e7 nonzeros at least 4 times faster in the sum with the reduced model in host memory and at least 5 times with device arrays (model: 9.5 and 14 times, 4.9 and 5.9 in the pessimistic setting); the 28 benchmark instances from 1e6 to 1e7 at least 3 times (model 6.7 to 10.8, pessimistic 4.3 to 5.3); the 67 from 2e5 to 1e6 at least 1.5 times (model 2.8 to 4.0, pessimistic 1.7 to 2.3); from device_min_nnz to 2e5 not slower in the sum; the host path (below device_min_nnz and without CUDA) at most 1.10 times today's in every size class; the HiGHS time rule (t at most 1.2 t(HiGHS) + 0.02 s) on every instance from 50,000 nonzeros.

8. Reductions. Against today's engine on the 240 and the 40: geometric mean of rows, columns and nonzeros (new over today) at most 1.001, every instance above 1.005 in any measure explained; the strength report for every instance whose hash differs; the Stage 1 and Stage 1c gates against the HiGHS references met. An instance where the new engine removes more is listed, not failed (A1).

9. Correctness. At least 1e9 random models through the host executor in batch order; at least 1e8 through the device in unions of screened blocks, with the oracle per block; unions with one planted infeasible or unbounded block; at least 1e7 standalone models on the device for statuses, budget stops and limited calls; the tolerance-edge profile; no oracle, structure, crush or second-presolve failure beyond those the triage of today's engine has classified. The structure check on the 240 and the 40 with both executors. Every wrong engine (rule, host step, kernel) is caught by a named check.

10. Overheads in every run: upload, download, hand-overs (count, bytes, seconds), column merges, rebuilds, record flushes, output, hash, landing, launches and read-backs, context creation apart, as columns of overheads.csv.

11. Assumptions. Every assumption made on the way is in ASSUMPTIONS.md with its switch; the result-changing switches have both positions on the host; the time-only switches are shown to change time only (hash equality with each forced low and high).

======== ORDER_WITH_REMAINING_RULES ========
State of the cheap column rules: the singleton-column rules are built and merged; simple probing on equations is built and in review (commit edd147a7e), as is the parallel-row repair; not built are dominance between parallel columns, dual bound strengthening, the single-lock alias, the merge of parallel columns, and the five low-worth rules (implied equation, implied integer with scaling, column stuffing, the single-equation extension, the dominated column). Their worth, measured with HiGHS on 237 of the 240 benchmark instances as remaining nonzeros against all cheap rules: 0.9726 with the singleton-column rules, 0.9826 with simple probing, 0.9932 with parallel columns, 0.9994 with the dual-fixing extensions, the rest from the five low-worth rules (1.0006 on the 240).

Order 1: the remaining rules first on the host, then the GPU work. This is the owner's decision A9 of 2026-11-27 ('the GPU state build is integrated after the cheap column rules'), taken before the directive. Benefit: the Stage 1c gate against HiGHS (0.99 of the reference with all cheap rules, the first goal of A10) is reached soonest, and every step form later has a reviewed sequential rule to be compared with. Cost: every remaining rule is written, reviewed and attacked by wrong engines twice, once in index order and once as a step. The size of one family is known from the singleton-column milestone: 593 lines of rule code, 34 of the 99 wrong engines, a validity review with four repairs, a campaign of 5.4 billion models. Rules written in index order also bring order-dependent data into the engine (the price cache of AS-303, marks and counters per call) that the state without history has to remove again. And all device work, and with it Stage 2, waits for the whole of Stage 1c; that defers device work, which the owner asked not to do without being asked.

Order 2: the GPU work first (M0 to M10), then the rules, each written once as a step on both executors. Benefit: one form, one review and one set of wrong engines per rule; the built rule set is complete on the GPU soonest. Cost: the gate against HiGHS with all cheap rules waits for the whole device work; until then the engine stays at about 0.983 of that reference in nonzeros (with simple probing); the step forms of the dual rules have no sequential rule of ours to be compared with, so their validity rests on the review alone, and the Stage 1c reviews broke several sequential guards by counterexample.

Recommendation: the GPU work first for the built rule set, with the remaining rules beside it and not after it. Concretely: finish the review of simple probing and of the parallel-row repair and the triage of the oracle failures; then M0 and M1. From M1 on every rule is two functions, an evaluation and an application. Each remaining rule is then written once in that form: first run on the host in the design's index order (apply_order = sequential), which is the form the Stage 1c designs were reviewed in and which gives the worth against the HiGHS ladder without waiting for any device work; then its batch form with its conflict rule, after the cell table and the validity review; then its kernels, which reuse the kernels of the family it resembles (parallel columns those of the parallel rows, dual bound strengthening the column kernel of propagation, the single-lock alias the alias pipeline, column stuffing the row step of the singleton family). The device track (M2, M6 to M10) and the host track (M3 to M5) run meanwhile. Cost of this order: M1 has to come first; a rule passes three gates instead of one, but with one body of arithmetic; two tracks touch the same rule files and have to be merged in turn. If only one track can run at a time, the GPU work goes first. This order reverses A9 and needs the owner's word.

======== REDUCTIONS_LOST ========
Measured, for the two families that were prototyped (row rules and bound propagation as batch rounds on the state at the start of the round, lists in ascending index, budgets tested between rounds; scratch copy of commit 04692babd, 240 benchmark dumps, host): nothing is lost. No instance has more rows, columns or nonzeros than today's engine. Two get smaller: momentum1 by 65 rows and 130 nonzeros, neos-1122047 by 2 rows and 4 nonzeros. Columns are equal on all 240. The reduced-model hash is equal on 234 of 240; the other four that differ (app1-2, satellites2-40, satellites2-60-fs, supportcase12) have the same sizes. The fixed point is reached on 234 in both. With the crossing rule added the result is the same on 240 of 240 (validity critic's run; I compared the files). Measured as hash-neutral alone, on the same dumps: activities as values (238 of 238 finished instances), the scale of row tightening as least common multiple (240 of 240, 0 of 8,842,639 searches differ); ascending lists change 2 of 240 hashes.

What these numbers do not see: the strength of the reduced model. On neos-1122047 the batch order turns 71,994 tightening findings into 7,238 and 72,403 propagation findings into 156,923 with 2 fewer rows, and 31 further instances have other finding counts with the same sizes. Whether bounds and coefficients are weaker or only reached by another path is not known; the strength report of the gates is there for that, because the owner's main goal is a stronger formulation.

Not measured, with where a loss is possible:
- Parallel rows by leader rounds: a bucket with more unlike classes than parallel_bucket_limit, or rows that are parallel only within the tolerance, can leave a pair unmerged that today's scan of four neighbours on each side finds, or the reverse. There are 433,141 exact comparisons for 395,950 rows with company in their bucket on the 240, so I expect few such buckets.
- The alias batch: a root coefficient that cancels in a partial sum today but not in the folded sum, or the reverse; single entries, either way.
- The singleton-column family in two steps: a unit that depends on a deletion of the same call waits a step; a loss needs a budget that ends the call first (20 calls stop on a budget on the 240 today, none of this family). The saved-entry limit as a prefix rule binds on none of the 240, the 31 and the nine in the stored node runs.
- Simple probing through the forest instead of the refusal of rows in list order: either way, on the 8 of the 240 instances where the rule finds something.
- Continuous bounds after the switch to explicit bounds: rounds take more and smaller steps, so the 16 steps per bound can end a chain earlier and the call reports limited. Seen on neos-1122047, as no loss.
- Budgets: a step is done in full, so a call can pass its limit by one step, and a long chain charges every round (square41 and square47 do 42.8 million more work units and finish a list that today stops halfway).
- The nine instances above 1e7 nonzeros and 14 of the 31 testbed instances were not run in any batch form.
- The nine rules that are not built have no measurement in either order.
Each of these is measured at the gate of the milestone that introduces it (probes in M0, gates in M3 to M5 and M11), on the 240 and the 40, per switch, with rows, columns and nonzeros, the strength report and the counts of stopped and limited calls. If a gate fails for one change, that change keeps its old position (for example neighbours for the parallel rows) and the rest goes on; the device then needs the old form of that one step, which is asked of the owner before it is built.

======== DEFECTS_TAKEN ========
--- 1
defect: Fatal under two lenses: 'bulk-first' leaves the state, every application, storage and records on the host (model: 1.9 times on the 240 benchmark instances, 1.7 times on the nine; at least 25 of 88.3 node seconds on the nine stay host work by construction).
how_fixed: It is not the frame. The state lives on the device and applications are device steps. Kept of it: the split of every visit into evaluation and application as milestone M1 with the audit, thresholds per family from the host cost per work unit, host memory from a presolve-owned resource with moved-in arrays and huge pages.

--- 2
defect: Serious (both wave designs): a singleton or forcing row is deleted in the step although its bound was cut by another row's bound that crosses it within the tolerance; the deleted row is then violated without limit (shown on the prototype: 5e-3 against a tolerance of 1e-6).
how_fixed: The crossing rule of the validity critic is part of the combine rule: a row that gave a bound is deleted only when the bounds written to its columns are at least as tight as its candidates, a forcing row only when every free column was written at its value; otherwise it is listed again and tested in the next step in its own units. Measured by the critic: the four models end infeasible, and 240 of 240 hashes equal the batch run on the benchmark dumps. The four models become unit tests.

--- 3
defect: Serious: maximum and minimum of doubles are not one result in bits (+0 and -0); only propagation had a tie rule.
how_fixed: Every reduction of bounds, sides and merged bounds is defined on pairs (value, source index): the better value, among values that compare equal the smaller source index, the winner's bits stored. That is the result of a loop in ascending source index with a strict comparison, on both executors.

--- 4
defect: Serious: reservation keys were not unique across row units and column units of the singleton-column family, so two units could hold one row; and the second half-wave for forcing columns had no defined state.
how_fixed: Forcing columns are a step of their own, evaluated on the state after the row units are applied and folded; row units write only their own row. The reservation rule requires keys that are unique over all units of a step (where two kinds share a step: 2 times the index plus the kind).

--- 5
defect: Serious: an entry added by a subtraction or a substitution waited for a rebuild that the rebuild rule might not trigger, so column-driven steps would read a column that lacks an entry.
how_fixed: New entries are merged into their columns at the end of the step that made them (designed in this step: the affected columns are merged in row order into free space at the end of the column store; the whole column store is re-sorted only when the batch is a large share of the nonzeros). Nothing is left pending.

--- 6
defect: Serious (both wave designs): the lane of an entry is its rank among the live entries of its row, but rows kept dead entries between rebuilds and no kernel was given the rank.
how_fixed: A live row never holds a dead entry: a step that deletes or renames an entry rewrites the row in place in the same step (possible because no rule of Stage 1 lets a row grow; I checked the two places where the engine adds an entry). The lane is then the slot position on both executors. Kernel tests cover rows that cross the 64-entry boundary through deletions.

--- 7
defect: Serious: a union of random models ends at the first infeasible or unbounded block (48.4% of 7.2 million campaign models end with a reduced model), so the union campaign would test almost nothing.

--- 8

how_fixed: A gate per rule in M11 before any code: a table of the cells the rule reads and writes, checked against its Stage 1c text, and a validity review of the step form. The index stays the reservation key; a hashed priority is not used. The conflict rules in this plan are marked as proposals. The reviews themselves are still open.

--- 9
defect: Serious: after the first hand-over the state returned to the device only at sixteen times the hand-over threshold, which left 34 to 84% of the structural work on the host between 1e5 and 1e6 nonzeros (84.4% of the rule work on the device over 114 instances of the 240).

======== DEFECTS_OPEN ========
- The nvcc check: whether nvcc compiles the shared headers with contraction off and returns the host's bits is unchecked in all three designs and here. The engine's only math calls are fabs, fmin, fmax, round, floor, ceil, fma, frexp, ldexp, llround and the classification functions (validity critic's grep), so the risk is contraction, the explicit fma and signed zero. It is the first item of M0; identity rests on it.
- No device step of this plan has been run. Every time is a model, and the fixed cost of a step differs by a factor of 1.5 to 2 between the designers' counts and the performance critic's. M0 measures mock steps; real steps are measured at M6 and M7.
- The nine instances above 1e7 nonzeros: no trace, no batch run. neos-4332810-sesia and dlr2 hold 44% of their time and their later calls hold 46 to 62% of the row-rule and propagation work; whether those calls are wide or deep is unknown. The rule 'no hand-back above the cap' rests on the 30 benchmark instances from 1e6 nonzeros.
- The host time of the batch engine. The prototype is at 1.12 times today's engine with two families converted (loaded Mac); lazy folds, rows rewritten in place and the sort of the substitution are not measured. Whether the 1.10 ceiling can be met is not shown.
- Reductions under three changed rules are not measured: parallel rows by leaders, the alias batch with ordered sums and rows in column order, the singleton-column family in two steps; nor simple probing through the forest. Activities as values are not finished on square41 and square47 and not run on the testbed.
- The validity of the step forms of the nine unbuilt rules. Only dual bound strengthening was checked by hand by one critic, and the implied-integer claim was read by another; the conflict rules for the other seven are proposals.
- The repairs designed in this synthesis are not prototyped and their cost is not measured: the rewrite of a row in the step that changes it, the merge of new entries into their columns, the singleton family in two steps, merges before subtraction pivots, the exact comparison of two cores by position.
- The strength of the reduced model under the batch order: on neos-1122047 the tightening findings fall from 71,994 to 7,238 with 2 fewer rows, and 31 further instances have other finding counts with equal sizes. Nobody compared bounds and coefficients.
- Whether a hand-back regime pays once its host copy is counted, and what happens to instances with long narrow chains above its cap (snp-02-004-104, irish-electricity). The 16 MiB cap is a provisional value from a model.
- The fold of very long rows by one block of 256 lanes is unmeasured (about 2 ms for a row of a million entries in the Stage 1b risk list); if it is too slow, the order of numerics.hpp changes for rows above 8,192 entries on both executors.
- Grace Hopper: nothing is measured, and host loops on managed pages whose preferred location is the device are a new cost. A second GPU type has never been run in this project.
- The cuOpt adapter as it exists takes and returns a host model and runs four cuOpt reductions on the host afterwards (adapter mode 3). Device arrays in and out, who owns the context and the pools, the byte budget next to cuOpt's early heuristics (about 120 bytes per nonzero during presolve) and the fixed costs on a busy GPU are planned for M12 and not designed.
- Node hours for the verification (device campaign, second GPU type, Grace Hopper) have no estimate; the rate of a device campaign is unknown.
- Today's engine has 18 oracle failures in 5.4 billion campaign models under triage, one of them a lost optimum caused by a bound of -3e19. Every gate 'against today's engine' means the engine after that triage; until it is in, the reference is not clean.
- That the minimum spanning forest under the row key gives today's tree edges at a first call, and that the reservation in one pass equals a loop in ascending key, are arguments, not tests.
- None of the designers read cuOpt's load-balanced kernels; what is liftable (classes by length, shared warps, the split of long lines, the CUDA graph) rests on the research step's reading, and that the code is unused rests on a grep of the CMake files.

======== REJECTED ========
--- 1
alternative: 'Bulk-first' as the plan: the device computes certificates of visits without effect, the host applies every finding in today's order and owns the state.
why: It does not meet the directive as read here: applications that are parallel (chain length 1 in every call of dual fixing, parallel rows and the doubleton rule on the 240) stay on the host, and so do the state, storage and records. Model: 1.9 times on the 240 benchmark instances and 1.7 times on the nine, 85% of the remaining time on the host; its own time criterion is missed. Its mirror of the host's layout history and its replay library would be thrown away by any engine that applies on the device. It remains the alternative if the owner wants today's bits.

--- 2
alternative: A second engine with its own state type and about 70 steps next to today's engine, with a wave presolve followed by a sequential presolve as scaffolding ('device-native').
why: Three control flows per rule during the project, two engines for good if the new one is slower on small models, and a composed presolve whose reductions are those of neither engine. The batch prototype shows that a step is a legal family of today's engine on today's state (700 patch lines for two families).

--- 3
alternative: The rewrite of 'one algorithm' in two large milestones without a switch back.
why: It breaks the rule that every assumption has a switch, bundles eight changes behind one reductions gate so that a difference cannot be traced, and pays the campaign, the wrong engines and the stored hashes twice. Two of the bundled changes are hash-neutral alone, so they can be merged one by one.

--- 4
alternative: The fused cascade wave (row rules, propagation, tightening and dual fixing in one wave) as the algorithm now.
why: It is unmeasured, it changes what a cascade pass, a call and max_cascade_passes mean (the prototype has 172 and 105 rounds in single family calls against a limit of 100 passes), and the measured batch form is the one with four family calls. Its gain is fixed cost in the class from 2e5 to 1e6 nonzeros. M0 measures it on the host (steps and reductions); it can be taken later with its own switch and gate, with the same kernels.

--- 5
alternative: Reproducing today's order of application on the device (prefix commits, replay of scripts in host order).
why: It is sequential work: for an arbitrary order the greedy problem is P-complete and a chain commits one unit per round (Blelloch, Fineman, Shun, read by the research step); PaPILO keeps its order by a sequential apply phase and reaches 1.9 times at 32 threads. Incremental activity bits are history and contradict one summation order.

--- 6
alternative: A host mirror up to 128 MiB with a return threshold of sixteen times the hand-over threshold.
why: It leaves 34 to 84% of the structural work on the host between 1e5 and 1e6 nonzeros and the mirror's creation (1.3 to 1.4 s on 106 instances of the 240) eats the gain; with it counted, staying on the device ties or wins from about 2e5 nonzeros in the model.

--- 7
alternative: A full rebuild after every structural phase with new entries, or a full re-sort of the column store after every alias batch.
why: On a large matrix with a small batch the device would do far more work than the host does today (hawaiiv10-130: 145 aliases in 3 calls, 51 ms per re-sort, up to 0.44 s of rebuilds against 0.05 s of storage time today).

--- 8
alternative: Physically binned device storage as the default.
why: A hand-over would be a permuted gather instead of a copy, host and device could be compared only through a hash, and the binned layout is itself unmeasured in place (AS-309). Index order with scheduling lists by length class gives the kernels the same classes; binned storage stays a benchmarked variant that cannot change a result.

--- 9
alternative: Hashed claim priorities, and a claim rule defined by a fixed point ('device-native').
why: A hashed priority gives another winner than the index order the Stage 1c designs were reviewed in, and the fixed-point wording has more than one solution. The single-pass reservation with the index as the key has one result; no long chain in index order is measured that would need hashing.

--- 10
alternative: The reservation table of DESIGN.md section 4 for alias trees and for row edits (one unit per row or per column cell per batch).
why: Only 32.0% of 280,666 trees fit a first batch and 13.2% of 151 doubleton calls need more than 64 batches (240), although no tree depends on another; tightening fits 23.2% of its units although its writes are distinct slots. The ordered fold and own-row writes need no exclusion.

--- 11
alternative: Floating-point atomics, library reductions for floating-point sums, and pointer jumping over the alias maps.
why: The owner forbids floating-point atomics (both published GPU presolvers and the GPU propagation paper use them); CCCL gives the same sum on every GPU type only for the plain reduction, not the segmented one; pointer jumping composes the maps in another association and can change the last bits. Pointer jumping is used on integers only.

--- 12
alternative: A campaign through the device on unions of unscreened random models.
why: A union ends at the first infeasible or unbounded block; with 48.4% of the models ending in a reduced model a union of 10 carries a model with probability about 0.0007.

--- 13
alternative: An equation with a singleton column serving all its targets in one step ('device-native').
why: Today the equation's core changes after the first subtraction, so it serves one target; all at once is valid as row operations but gives the singleton column one entry per target and departs from the reviewed rule without a measured need.

--- 14
alternative: Reaching 'complete' by a high size threshold fixed at the last milestone by smallest summed time alone.
why: With a threshold of 1e6 nonzeros 117 of the 147 benchmark instances from 5e4 nonzeros would never use the GPU and every acceptance list would still pass. The threshold is bounded and needs the owner's consent above 200,000.

--- 15
alternative: Dynamic sparse-matrix structures on the device (per-line blocks, hash tables).
why: No rule of Stage 1 lets a row grow, and a column grows in two rules only; a row rewrite in place and a column merge at the end of a step are enough. Neither published GPU presolver uses a dynamic structure (research step).

--- 16
alternative: Writing the nine remaining rules first as sequential rules and porting them afterwards, or holding them until all device work is done.
why: Twice the code, reviews and wrong engines in the first case; the gate against HiGHS at the very end in the second. With evaluation and application split, a rule is written once and both orders run it.
